Foxes in the Garden

Five foxes asleep in south London

September 2022, 3 min read

Draft, for review

On this page

  1. Foxes in London
  2. Five asleep

In the late summer of 2022 there were foxes in the garden of the flat in Southwark, in south London: one scratching an itch in the evening sun in August, and, one morning in September, five of them curled up fast asleep together on a roof, most likely a family with this year's cubs among them.

On the evening of Sunday 21 August 2022 there was a fox in the garden of the flat in Southwark, among the leaves of a shrub, scratching its neck with a hind foot, eyes half shut and nose pointed up into the leaves. On the morning of Thursday 8 September, at ten o’clock, there were five, curled up asleep together on a roof in the garden, several of them with their noses tucked into their tails.

Southwark is an inner London borough on the south bank of the Thames, its northern end just across the river from the City. It isn’t the countryside, but foxes don’t seem to mind.

Foxes in London

Foxes began living in British towns in the 1930s, in the new suburbs, where low-density houses with big gardens suited them, and they’ve been in London since the 1930s and 1940s. The number usually given for London is about 10,000. They live in family groups: a dog fox and a vixen, often a vixen or two who don’t breed, perhaps the previous year’s young, and that year’s cubs.

Five asleep

Fox cubs are born in March and come out of the earth for the first time in late April. By September, adults and cubs alike have almost finished growing their winter coats. The cubs don’t leave home until the autumn, the young males first, from October. So five foxes asleep together on a September morning are most likely a family, with this year’s cubs among them, though which are the cubs, I can’t tell.

For most of the year, foxes don’t sleep underground. They use an earth to raise their cubs and to shelter from bad weather, and otherwise they lie up above ground, in thick cover or, in towns, often on the roofs of sheds, where they can see trouble coming. These five were in the open, with the garden’s shrubs hanging over them.
