A Weekend on Skye

A sunrise over Raasay, the Cuillin, and a buzzard by the Storr

May 2023, 5 min read

Draft, for review

On this page

  2. The Old Man of Storr

Cal and I spent a weekend on the Isle of Skye in May 2023, on a trip north in the van. On the Sunday I got up at five, opened the door of the van, and was greeted by the most amazing sunrise over Raasay and the sea. Here are that sunrise, the Cuillin from our drone, and a buzzard landing right next to the Old Man of Storr.

The Highlands and Skye, part 2 of 3

Cal and I came over to Skye in the van on the afternoon of Saturday 6 May 2023, after stopping at the Glenfinnan Viaduct on the way north. We spent the weekend on the island, and on the Monday we came home through Glen Coe.

Sunrise over Raasay

On the Saturday night the van was parked near the Storr, on the east coast of the Trotternish peninsula, above the Sound of Raasay. On the Sunday morning I got up at about five to have a pee, opened the door of the van, and was greeted by the most amazing sunrise over Raasay and the sea.

The sun rose at about twenty past five, in the north-east, over the mainland across the Inner Sound. These two panoramas are from our drone, over the coast below the Storr, at about a quarter to six. The low islands are Rona, in front of the sun, and the north end of Raasay. The jagged mountains on the skyline to the right of the sun are Torridon’s, Beinn Alligin, Beinn Eighe and Liathach, about 40 kilometres away.

Turned south, the drone looked down the Sound, with Raasay on the left, the flat top of Dùn Caan its highest point at 443 metres, and the hills of central Skye far beyond. On the right is Loch Leathan, dammed in the early 1950s for the little hydro-electric power station on the shore of Bearreraig Bay; its pipes run down the cliff at the bottom of the picture.

The Old Man of Storr

The Storr, 719 metres, is the highest point of the Trotternish ridge, an escarpment of lava flows about 60 million years old lying on older, softer Jurassic rocks. Where those gave way, huge blocks of lava slid down and out from the cliffs, and Trotternish is the largest continuous area of landslides in the British Isles. The Old Man of Storr, a pinnacle of basalt about 50 metres high, is what weathering has left of one of the Storr’s blocks, which slid about 6,500 years ago.

That evening, at about twenty to eight, I photographed a buzzard landing in the grass right next to the Old Man.

It’s a common buzzard, Britain’s commonest bird of prey. It usually hunts from a perch, dropping onto voles, rabbits and other small animals, and it walks the ground for earthworms too. Persecution and pesticides cut its numbers right down in the last century, but by 2016 there were about 63,000 pairs in the UK. It’s in Birds of Prey on Thalassa too.

The Cuillin

The other two photographs are from the Saturday, the day we arrived.

This one is from our drone over Sconser, on Loch Sligachan, at twenty past five: the island of Scalpay across the sea on the left, the quarry on our shore, and the A87 climbing the glen in the middle, on its way south to Loch Ainort. Beyond are the rounded hills above Broadford, then the jagged peaks of Blà Bheinn and its neighbours, and on the right the Red Cuillin’s Beinn Dearg Mhòr and Glamaig, 775 metres, the big cone with a path up it. In 1899 a Gurkha soldier, Harkbir Thapa, ran barefoot from the Sligachan Inn to its top in 37 minutes, and back down in 18.

The Black Cuillin are gabbro, dark and jagged; the Red Cuillin are granite, rounded and covered in scree. Both have been worn down from the roots of a volcano that was active about 60 million years ago.

Two hours later, on the west coast, our drone was over the north shore of Loch Harport, by Gesto, near Bracadale. The loch runs away south-east towards Carbost, with gorse in flower along its shore, and beyond the moors the Black Cuillin line the skyline, from Sgùrr nan Gillean on the left to Sgùrr Dearg on the right, with Marsco and Blà Bheinn further left.

The photographs

I took the buzzard with my Nikon D850 and Sigma 150–600 mm lens, and the rest from our drone. The sunrise, the view south and the view from above Sconser are each stitched from several frames, and the view south also merges darker and lighter exposures of the same view.
