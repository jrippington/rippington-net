A Weekend in the Lake District

Buttermere, Moss Force, and Ullswater

February 2023, 4 min read

Cal and I spent a weekend in February in the Lake District, in the van. On the Sunday morning we were beside Buttermere just after sunrise, and I flew our drone over Moss Force, the waterfall at the top of Newlands Hause, and then high over Ullswater from above Aira Force. On the Friday, Cal photographed a ewe and her lamb, and I photographed a lone tree in the afternoon light.

Cal and I spent the weekend of 17 to 19 February 2023 in the Lake District, in the van, our camper. On the Friday, Cal photographed a ewe with her lamb, and I photographed a lone tree by a dry-stone wall. On the Sunday morning the van was on a verge beside Buttermere a few minutes after sunrise, our drone was over Moss Force at the top of Newlands Hause before eight, and by ten it was high over Ullswater, above Aira Force.

A ewe, a lamb, and a tree

The ewe was lying on the wet grass with her lamb pressed against her side, the lamb’s eyes half closed and hers on Cal. Both have the farmer’s blue marks on their wool, and she has a tag in each ear.

She isn’t a Herdwick, the Lake District’s own breed: Herdwick lambs are born black, and hers is white. Nor is she a Swaledale, another breed common here, whose ewes have horns. Most Herdwicks spend the winter out on the open fell, each ewe keeping to her heaf, her own small patch of it, which her lambs learn from her. The dry-stone walls that cross the valleys are here because of the sheep.

That afternoon I photographed the tree, alone on its knoll above a walled track, with the sun low behind the fell on the left.

Buttermere

Buttermere is a small lake, about 2 kilometres long, between High Stile and Robinson, with Fleetwith Pike and Haystacks at its south-eastern end. Its name is Old English for “butter lake”, perhaps for the fertile land at either end. It drains through a short stream, Buttermere Dubs, into Crummock Water.

Moss Force

Twenty-five minutes later our drone was up again at Newlands Hause, the pass over to the Newlands valley, 333 metres up. Just below the top, on the south side, is Moss Force. Moss Beck rises at about 540 metres on Buttermere Moss, a boggy shelf on Robinson’s western slopes, drops off the Moss’s northern edge in three falls, about 100 metres in all, and then runs away from Buttermere altogether, down into the Newlands valley. The drone was hovering about level with the top of the falls.

The top fall is the highest of the three, and a rock in the middle of it splits the water in two. Samuel Taylor Coleridge, who explored the falls in 1802, called the upper one “so fearfully savage, & black, & jagged, that it tears the flood to pieces” in a letter to Sara Hutchinson. I photographed the rock with the long lens, at 600 mm.

Ullswater

Two hours later the drone was up again on the other side of Keswick, over the woods by Aira Force, looking south-west along Ullswater, the second largest lake in the Lake District. The picture at the top of this page looks along its southern reach. Place Fell is the dark fell on the left. At the head of the lake, St Sunday Crag has its top in the cloud, with the Helvellyn range hidden behind it and Glenridding below, and Sheffield Pike is on the right.

The near shore runs on from below the drone to Glencoyne Bay, where William and Dorothy Wordsworth walked on 15 April 1802. Dorothy’s journal for that day tells of a few daffodils close to the water in the woods beyond Gowbarrow Park, and two years later William made them into “I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud”.

The photographs

The two pictures from the air are panoramas, stitched from several frames from our drone, a DJI Mavic 2 Pro; Moss Force’s runs up and down rather than across. The picture of the upper fall is stitched from several frames too, taken on my Nikon D850 with a Sigma 150–600 mm at 600 mm, at a fifth of a second: long enough to draw the water out into threads. The tree is an HDR picture, merged from bracketed frames on the D850, which keeps detail in both the bright sky and the shadows. Cal took the ewe and her lamb on a Nikon D50; they’re in the Hoofed Mammals gallery on Thalassa too, with a note.
