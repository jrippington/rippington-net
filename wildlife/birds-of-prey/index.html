The birds of prey I've photographed so far: Bob, a white-tailed eagle at the Welsh Mountain Zoo, above Colwyn Bay, who sat with Cal and me for hours one Sunday afternoon in October 2023, and a wild common buzzard coming in to land by the Old Man of Storr, on Skye.

My photographs of birds of prey are below, a species at a time, each with a note on the bird and the day. Bob is from a day at the Welsh Mountain Zoo with Cal: it’s our name for him, not the zoo’s. The buzzard is from a weekend on Skye.

Common buzzard

Buteo buteo1 photograph

Britain's commonest bird of prey, found almost everywhere, often soaring over fields and woods with its wings held in a shallow V and calling a mewing pee-yow. Its numbers have recovered strongly since persecution, pesticides and myxomatosis cut them down.

White-tailed eagle

Haliaeetus albicilla1 photograph

Britain's largest bird of prey, with wings up to about 2.5 metres across. Persecuted to extinction in Britain, where the last native bird was shot in 1918, it was brought back from Norway to the Isle of Rum from 1975, and has been released on the Isle of Wight since 2019. An adult has a yellow beak and eyes, a pale head and a white tail.
