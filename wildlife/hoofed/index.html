Hoofed Mammals

Deer, ponies and sheep

The hoofed animals I've photographed so far: the fallow and red deer of Bushy Park, the hill ponies of Dartmoor, and a ewe and her lamb in the Lake District.

My photographs of hoofed animals are below, a species at a time, each with a note on the animal and the day. Each links to the journey on Arda it came from: Bushy Park, Dartmoor and the Lake District.

Fallow deer

Dama dama4 photographs

First brought to Britain by the Romans, and again around the time of the Normans after the Roman herds had died out, fallow deer now live in parks and woods across England and Wales. Their coats run from dark through spotted chestnut to white, and the bucks grow broad, flattened antlers, shed and grown again every year.

Red deer

Cervus elaphus1 photograph

Britain's largest native land mammal. The stags grow a new set of antlers every year, and in the autumn rut they roar, gather hinds and fight off their rivals.

Dartmoor hill pony

Equus ferus caballus3 photographs

The ponies that graze Dartmoor freely all year round. Every one belongs to a farmer, and each autumn they're gathered in the drifts. Many are patched hill ponies rather than the pure Dartmoor breed, which is never patched: bay, brown, black, grey, chestnut or roan.

Sheep

Ovis aries1 photograph

Tamed in the Near East more than 10,000 years ago, sheep now graze the hills of Britain almost everywhere, the fells of the Lake District among them. Most lambs are born between late winter and spring.
