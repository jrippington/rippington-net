The big cats I've photographed so far: the Sumatran tigers and the snow leopard at the Welsh Mountain Zoo, above Colwyn Bay, on a Sunday afternoon in October 2023.

My photographs of the big cats are below, a species at a time, each with a note on the animal and the day. These are from a day at the Welsh Mountain Zoo with Cal, where the tigers are a pair, Kinnara and Budi.

Sumatran tiger

Panthera tigris sondaica3 photographs

The smallest tigers alive, found in the wild only on the Indonesian island of Sumatra: a male weighs about 100 to 140 kilograms. They're critically endangered, about 600 of them by an estimate published in 2017, as their forests are cleared for plantations and they're snared and poached. Europe's zoos breed them in a shared programme.

Snow leopard

Panthera uncia1 photograph

A cat of the high mountains of twelve countries in Central and South Asia, usually 3,000 to 5,000 metres up, with a tail almost as long as its body that helps it balance on steep rock. Unlike the other big cats, it can't roar. It's listed as vulnerable, with perhaps 2,700 to 3,400 adults in the wild, though the number is disputed.
