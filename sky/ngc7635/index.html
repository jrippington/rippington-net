A shell of gas about 9 light-years across, blown by the wind of one hot star, about 9,200 light-years away in Cassiopeia, in the Perseus Arm of the Milky Way. The star sits well off its centre, and whether it's a bubble or a bow shock is still argued.

The Bubble Nebula, NGC 7635, is a shell of gas blown by the wind of one hot star, BD+60 2522, about 9,200 light-years away in Cassiopeia. The star sits well off the shell’s centre, and is moving that way, so some astronomers think the Bubble may be a bow shock, like the wave in front of a moving boat; it isn’t settled.
