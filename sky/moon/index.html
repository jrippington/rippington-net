The Moon

Its face up close

Our Moon, 3,475 km across and on average 384,400 km away: close enough for a telescope in a garden to show its craters, mountains and seas, and the places where spacecraft came down. My pictures from June 2024, each with a page of its own.

The Moon is 3,475 km across, a little over a quarter of the Earth’s width, and on average 384,400 km away. It turns once for each trip round us, so it always shows us the same face. Its craters and mountains show best near the line between day and night, where the shadows are longest, and that line sweeps across the face once a month.

All of these pictures are from my garden in Oxfordshire, with the 250 mm Newtonian and a small planetary camera, in June 2024. I was just trying the camera out, and I liked the results, so I kept them.

My pictures of it are below, by framing. Those I’ve written about open on pages of their own, where you can explore them at full resolution.
