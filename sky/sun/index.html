Our own star, about 150 million kilometres away, and its corona, its outer atmosphere, which can only be seen when the Moon covers it.

The Sun is our own star, about 150 million kilometres away. Its outer atmosphere, the corona, is a million times fainter than its surface, and can only be seen when the Moon covers it at a total eclipse, along with the prominences, clouds of glowing gas held up off its edge. My picture of the total eclipse of 12 August 2026, from Soria in Spain, is the first here.

My pictures of it are below, by framing. Those I’ve written about open on pages of their own, where you can explore them at full resolution.
