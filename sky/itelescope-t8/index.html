The telescopes, Optica

iTelescope T8

A wide-field refractor at Siding Spring

March 2022, 2 min read

On this page

  1. The telescope and the camera
  2. In numbers

T8 is a Takahashi FSQ-106ED, a 106 mm refractor built for photography, with a large CCD camera behind it, on a Paramount mount at Siding Spring Observatory in Australia. Its square frame is almost 4° on a side, nearly eight full Moons across, so it's for the big nebulae and star fields.

I started using iTelescope in 2022-2023, primarily to explore the southern skies while I was living in Central London. T8 is one of iTelescope’s telescopes at Siding Spring Observatory, in New South Wales, 31° south of the equator.

The telescope and the camera

The FSQ is a Petzval design: four lenses, two at the front and two near the back, which between them bring red, green, and blue light to the same focus (that’s what makes it an apochromat) and keep the stars sharp across a large, flat field. A flat field is what a big sensor needs, and T8 has one: a KAF-16803 CCD in an FLI MicroLine camera, 4096 pixels square, each 9 µm across, so the chip is about 37 mm on a side.

Each pixel covers 3.5 arcseconds of sky. The camera is monochrome, so the colour comes from filters, with a set of exposures through each: red, green, and blue; a clear luminance filter; narrowband filters for the light of glowing hydrogen, oxygen, and sulphur; and an exoplanet filter (EXO), made for measuring the slight dimming of a star as a planet crosses it.

In numbers

TelescopeTakahashi FSQ-106ED, a four-element Petzval apochromat
Aperture106 mm
Focal length530 mm, f/5
CameraFLI MicroLine 16803, monochrome CCD, cooled to −25 °C in summer and −30 °C in winter
SensorKAF-16803, 4096 × 4096 pixels (16.8 megapixels), each 9 µm across
Field of view3.98° × 3.98°
Pixel scale3.5″ per pixel
FiltersAstrodon Series 2: L, R, G, B, Hα, OIII, SII, and EXO
ExposuresUp to 600 seconds recommended, binned 1 × 1 or 2 × 2
MountParamount PME
GuidingExternal
SiteSiding Spring Observatory, New South Wales, Australia: 1,122 m up, at 31.3° S, 149.1° E
