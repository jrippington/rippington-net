The telescopes, Optica

150 mm Newtonian

A portable Quattro 150P on a ZWO AM5

September 2023, 3 min read

On this page

  1. The telescope
  2. The camera and its filters
  3. The mount and the guiding
  4. In numbers

My 150 mm Newtonian is a Sky-Watcher Quattro 150P on a ZWO AM5 mount: a portable instrument, unlike the 250 mm in its shed. It carries the same Altair colour camera, and with its coma corrector it works at about 519 mm and f/3.5, taking in about 2.6° by 1.7°, five full Moons by three.

The telescope

The Quattro 150P is a Newtonian, like the 250 mm, but a smaller and faster one: a 150 mm primary mirror with a focal length of 600 mm, which makes it f/4. Like any fast Newtonian it shows coma towards the edges of the frame, where stars are drawn out into small comet shapes, so I use the coma corrector Sky-Watcher supply with it. It’s sold as 0.81×, but the stars in my pictures put the telescope at about 519 mm and f/3.5 with it, a factor of about 0.86 (a corrector’s reduction depends on how far behind it the camera sits). It still collects the light of faint, spread-out targets quickly, and its short tube keeps the whole instrument compact.

The camera and its filters

It carries the same camera as the 250 mm, an Altair Hypercam 26C TEC, cooled, usually to −10 °C, with Sony’s IMX571 colour sensor of 6248 by 4176 pixels, each 3.76 µm. At 519 mm each pixel covers 1.5 arcseconds of sky, and the frame about 2.6° by 1.7°, about 1.7 times as wide as the 250 mm’s.

There’s no filter wheel and no rotator on this one. Filters go in a filter drawer, a slot in front of the camera that takes one at a time and is changed by hand. They’re usually the three from the 250 mm’s wheel: Antlia’s 5 nm dual-band filters for hydrogen-alpha and oxygen III, and for hydrogen-beta and sulphur II, and the Altair TRI-RGB. The 250 mm’s page says what each does on a colour sensor.

The mount and the guiding

The mount is a ZWO AM5, a compact equatorial mount with strain-wave gearing, which carries a telescope this size without a counterweight. With the short tube, that keeps the whole instrument small and light enough to carry. It’s guided through a 60 mm SVBONY guide scope with a ZWO ASI120MM camera, and it stands on either a carbon-fibre or a steel tripod; the carbon-fibre one, in the photograph, also carries my portable DSLR rig.
