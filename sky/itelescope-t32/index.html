The telescopes, Optica

iTelescope T32

A 17-inch reflector with a full-frame camera

March 2022, 2 min read

On this page

  1. The telescope and the camera
  2. In numbers

T32 is a PlaneWave CDK17, a 0.43 m reflector, with a full-frame CMOS camera, on PlaneWave's Ascension 200HR mount at Siding Spring Observatory in Australia. Its frame is 42′ by 28′, a little wider than the full Moon, at about half an arcsecond per binned pixel: close in on galaxies and small nebulae.

I started using iTelescope in 2022-2023, primarily to explore the southern skies while I was living in Central London. T32 is one of iTelescope’s telescopes at Siding Spring Observatory, in New South Wales, 31° south of the equator.

The telescope and the camera

The CDK17 is PlaneWave’s 17-inch corrected Dall-Kirkham, a mirror telescope whose two lenses before the focus keep its field flat and its stars round to the edges. At f/6.8 it has a focal length of 2,912 mm.

It carries the same camera as T17, a ZWO ASI6200MM Pro with Sony’s full-frame IMX455 sensor, 9576 by 6388 pixels of 3.76 µm, run binned 2 × 2 so that each square of four pixels counts as one, at 0.53 arcseconds. The difference is the mount: PlaneWave’s Ascension 200HR tracks well enough that T32 runs without guiding. It has photometric V and Ic filters as well as colour and narrowband ones.

Before the ZWO it had a CCD camera with a KAF-16803 sensor, 4096 by 4096 pixels of 9 µm, each covering 0.63 arcseconds, in a field 43 arcminutes square, and my picture of the Pencil Nebula, in March 2023, was taken with it. iTelescope’s records show two cameras with that sensor: an FLI ProLine 16803 until at least December 2022, and a Moravian G4-16000 from March 2023. The ZWO had replaced it by August 2025.
