The telescopes, Optica

iTelescope T71

A fast Newtonian astrograph in Chile

March 2022, 2 min read

On this page

  1. The telescope and the camera
  2. In numbers

T71 is a Takahashi Epsilon-180ED, a 180 mm reflector working at f/2.8, with a cooled monochrome CMOS camera, on a Paramount MyT at Deep Sky Chile in the Río Hurtado Valley. Its frame is 2.7° by 1.8°, about five full Moons by three and a half, and at f/2.8 it gathers faint light quickly.

I started using iTelescope in 2022-2023, primarily to explore the southern skies while I was living in Central London. T71 is at Deep Sky Chile, at the same site as T70, 30.5° south.

The telescope and the camera

The Epsilon is a Newtonian, as my 250 mm and 150 mm telescopes are, but one made for photography: its primary mirror is hyperbolic rather than parabolic, and a corrector lens near the focus flattens the field and keeps the stars round to the edges. With a 180 mm mirror and a focal length of 500 mm it works at f/2.8, so each pixel collects light about four times as fast as it would at f/5.6.

The camera is the same as T70’s, a ZWO ASI2600MM Pro, 6248 by 4176 pixels of 3.76 µm, each covering 1.55 arcseconds here. Like T70 it’s monochrome, with colour and 5 nm narrowband filters, and it’s guided through a small separate guide scope.

Aperture180 mm
Focal length500 mm, f/2.8
CameraZWO ASI2600MM Pro, monochrome CMOS, cooled to −10 °C
SensorSony IMX571, 6248 × 4176 pixels (26 megapixels), each 3.76 µm across
Field of view2.69° × 1.80°
Pixel scale1.55″ per pixel
FiltersAstrodon Gen 2 I-Series: L, R, G, B; Hα, OIII, and SII, each 5 nm wide
ExposuresUp to 600 seconds, binned 1 × 1 or 2 × 2
MountParamount MyT
GuidingQHY mini guide scope (f/4.3), with a Lodestar Pro guide camera
SiteDeep Sky Chile, Río Hurtado Valley, Chile: 1,710 m up, at 30.5° S, 70.9° W
