A cloud of glowing hydrogen about 2,600 light-years away in Cygnus, about 90 light-years across, lit by one hot star hidden behind the dark cloud that cuts it into the shape of the continent. Its brightest edge is the Cygnus Wall.

The North America Nebula, NGC 7000, is a cloud of hydrogen about 2,600 light-years away in Cygnus, lit by one hot star, the Bajamar Star, which the dark Gulf of Mexico cloud hides from us. The continent’s outline is drawn by that dark dust in front of it, and its brightest edge, along Mexico’s Pacific coast, is the Cygnus Wall.
