The telescopes, Optica

iTelescope T70

A camera lens under the southern sky

March 2022, 2 min read

On this page

  1. The lens and the camera
  2. In numbers

T70 is one of the remote telescopes at iTelescope.net, and its optics are a camera lens: a Samyang 135 mm, with a cooled monochrome camera behind it, on a Paramount ME mount at Deep Sky Chile, in the Río Hurtado Valley. Its frame is about nineteen full Moons across, so it's for the big things: the largest nebulae, and long stretches of the Milky Way.

I started using iTelescope in 2022-2023, primarily to explore the southern skies while I was living in Central London. iTelescope.net rents out time on telescopes at observatories around the world, and several of them are in the southern hemisphere.

From London, a lot of that sky is out of reach. At 51.5° north, anything more than about 38.5° south of the celestial equator never rises, and that’s about a fifth of the whole sky. Deep Sky Chile is at 30.5° south, where the Magellanic Clouds, two small neighbouring galaxies, never set.

The lens and the camera

The Samyang is a lens made for ordinary cameras, and opens to f/2. iTelescope run it stopped down to f/3.5. That costs light: the working aperture, the focal length divided by the focal ratio, falls from about 67 mm to about 38 mm, which lets in about a third as much. Stopping a lens down usually sharpens the stars towards the edges of the frame; even so, iTelescope note a little coma, stars drawn out into tiny comet shapes, in the far corners.

Behind the lens is a ZWO ASI2600MM Pro, a monochrome camera cooled to −10 °C, which keeps down the noise its own warmth would add. A monochrome sensor measures how much light reaches each pixel but not what colour it is, so the colour comes from filters, with a set of exposures through each: red, green, and blue for natural colour; a clear luminance filter, which passes all three; and three narrowband filters, which pass only the light of glowing hydrogen, oxygen, or sulphur.

In numbers

LensSamyang 135 mm f/2, stopped down to f/3.5
Working apertureAbout 38 mm
CameraZWO ASI2600MM Pro, monochrome, cooled to −10 °C
SensorSony IMX571 CMOS, 6248 × 4176 pixels (26 megapixels), each 3.76 µm across
Field of view9.97° × 6.66°
Pixel scaleAbout 5.8″ per pixel
FiltersAstrodon Gen 2 E-Series: L, R, G, B, Hα, OIII, and SII
ExposuresUp to 300 seconds, binned 1 × 1 or 2 × 2
MountParamount ME
GuidingZWO 30 mm f/4 guide scope, with a Lodestar SX2 guide camera
SiteDeep Sky Chile, Río Hurtado Valley, Chile: 1,710 m up, at 30.5° S, 70.9° W
