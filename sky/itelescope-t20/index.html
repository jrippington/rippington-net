The telescopes, Optica

iTelescope T20

A wide-field refractor with a colour camera

March 2022, 2 min read

On this page

  1. The telescope and the camera
  2. In numbers

T20 is a Takahashi FSQ-106ED, a 106 mm refractor built for photography, with a one-shot colour CMOS camera, on a Paramount ME at the Utah Desert Remote Observatory. Its frame is 3.9° by 2.6°, about seven and a half full Moons by five, for big nebulae, clusters, and comets.

I started using iTelescope in 2022-2023, primarily to explore the southern skies while I was living in Central London. T20 is in the other hemisphere, at the Utah Desert Remote Observatory near Beryl Junction, Utah, 37.7° north and 1,570 m up.

The telescope and the camera

The telescope is the FSQ-106ED, the four-lens Petzval astrograph also on T8 at Siding Spring, which keeps a wide field flat and its colours together.

What sets T20 apart is its camera, a ZWO ASI2400MC Pro, which takes colour pictures in a single exposure. Each of its 6072 by 4042 pixels sits behind a tiny red, green, or blue filter, in a repeating pattern called a Bayer matrix, so one frame records all three. That’s quicker than taking red, green, and blue in turn through a monochrome camera, at some cost in sharpness and sensitivity, since each pixel sees only one colour. Its large 5.94 µm pixels each cover 2.32 arcseconds. In place of colour filters it has Optolong’s L-Pro, which cuts out much of the light of street lamps, and their L-Ultimate, which passes only narrow bands around the light of hydrogen and oxygen; there are separate narrowband filters too.

In numbers

TelescopeTakahashi FSQ-106ED, a four-element Petzval apochromat
Aperture106 mm
Focal length530 mm, f/5
CameraZWO ASI2400MC Pro, one-shot colour CMOS, cooled to −15 °C
Sensor6072 × 4042 pixels (24.5 megapixels), each 5.94 µm across
Field of view3.89° × 2.60°
Pixel scale2.32″ per pixel
FiltersOptolong L-Pro and L-Ultimate; Astrodon Hα and SII (3 nm) and OIII (5 nm)
ExposuresUp to 600 seconds, unbinned
MountParamount ME
GuidingNone: unguided
SiteUtah Desert Remote Observatory, Beryl Junction, Utah, USA: 1,570 m up, at 37.7° N, 113.7° W
