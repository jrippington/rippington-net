The Tarantula Nebula

A starburst in the Large Magellanic Cloud

The biggest and brightest star-forming region in our Local Group of galaxies, in the Large Magellanic Cloud, about 162,000 light-years away. From dark southern skies it shows to the naked eye as a fuzzy star.

The Tarantula Nebula, 30 Doradus, is in the Large Magellanic Cloud, a small galaxy that orbits our own. Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille catalogued it from the Cape of Good Hope in 1751 or 1752, and the cluster at its heart, R136, holds the most massive stars known.
