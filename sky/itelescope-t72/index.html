The telescopes, Optica

iTelescope T72

A 20-inch reflector in Chile

March 2022, 2 min read

On this page

  1. The telescope and the camera
  2. In numbers

T72 is a PlaneWave CDK20, a 0.51 m reflector at f/6.8, with a CCD camera, on PlaneWave's L-500 mount at Deep Sky Chile in the Río Hurtado Valley. Its frame is 27′ by 21.5′, smaller than the full Moon, and each pixel covers just 0.36″: the narrowest view of the iTelescope telescopes I've used, for small galaxies and planetary nebulae.

I started using iTelescope in 2022-2023, primarily to explore the southern skies while I was living in Central London. T72 is at Deep Sky Chile, at the same site as T70 and T71, 30.5° south.

The telescope and the camera

The CDK20 is a corrected Dall-Kirkham: a 20-inch concave primary mirror, a convex secondary, and two lenses before the focus that keep the field flat and the stars round to its edges. T72 uses it at its full focal length, 3,411 mm, inside a black shroud that keeps stray light off the mirrors, on PlaneWave’s L-500, a direct-drive mount, without guiding.

The camera is an FLI ML16200, a monochrome CCD of 4500 by 3600 pixels, each 6 µm and covering 0.36 arcseconds, finer than the blur the atmosphere usually adds. Its filters are Chroma’s: colour and narrowband, the photometric U, B, V, R, and I, and a blank.
