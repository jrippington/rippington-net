The debris of a star that exploded about 11,000 years ago, as seen from here, about 940 light-years away: one of the nearest supernova remnants known, 8 degrees across.

The Vela supernova remnant is the expanding debris of a star that exploded about 11,000 years ago, as seen from here, spread across 8 degrees of the southern sky. Its brightest piece, the Pencil Nebula, was found by John Herschel from the Cape of Good Hope in 1835, and the pulsar at its heart, found in 1968, was some of the first evidence that supernovae leave neutron stars behind.
