The Veil Nebula, the Cygnus Loop, about 2,400 light-years away in Cygnus: the shell of a star that exploded about 20,000 years ago, its blast wave still glowing where it runs into clouds of gas.

The Veil Nebula is the brightest part of the Cygnus Loop, the shell of gas swept up by a star that exploded about 20,000 years ago, about 2,400 light-years away in Cygnus. The shell is about 120 light-years across, nearly three degrees on the sky; its filaments glow where the blast wave runs into clouds of gas, oxygen’s blue-green just ahead of hydrogen’s red, and they look so fine because they’re rippled sheets of gas seen edge on. William Herschel found it in September 1784.
