The telescopes, Optica

iTelescope T17

A 17-inch reflector at Siding Spring

March 2022, 2 min read

On this page

  1. The telescope and the camera
  2. In numbers

T17 is a PlaneWave CDK17, a 0.43 m reflector, with a full-frame CMOS camera, on a Paramount PME at Siding Spring Observatory in Australia. Its frame is 42′ by 28′, a little wider than the full Moon, with each pixel covering about half an arcsecond, so it's for the detail in galaxies, small nebulae, and the bright cores of big ones.

I started using iTelescope in 2022-2023, primarily to explore the southern skies while I was living in Central London. T17 is one of iTelescope’s telescopes at Siding Spring Observatory, in New South Wales, 31° south of the equator.

The telescope and the camera

The CDK17 is PlaneWave’s 17-inch corrected Dall-Kirkham: a concave primary mirror and a convex secondary, with two lenses before the focus that keep the stars round out to the edges of the field. At f/6.8 its focal length is 2,939 mm, nearly 3 m of light path folded into a short open truss.

The camera is a ZWO ASI6200MM Pro, built round Sony’s IMX455, a full-frame (36 by 24 mm) monochrome CMOS sensor of 9576 by 6388 pixels, each 3.76 µm. At this focal length a single pixel would cover only about a quarter of an arcsecond, finer than the blur the atmosphere adds, so iTelescope run it binned 2 × 2: each square of four pixels is combined into one, covering 0.53 arcseconds.

Until 2024 it had a CCD camera instead, and that’s the one my picture of the Southern Pinwheel was taken with, in March 2023: an FLI ProLine PL4710, with an e2v CCD47-10 sensor of 1024 by 1024 pixels, each 13 µm. At the focal length iTelescope gave then, 2,912 mm, each pixel covered 0.92 arcseconds, and the whole frame only 15.5 arcminutes square, about half the width of the full Moon. The ZWO replaced it between March and June 2024.
