The Eagle Nebula, about 5,600 light-years away in Serpens: glowing hydrogen round a young cluster, whose light has carved the Pillars of Creation.

The Eagle Nebula, M16, is a cloud of glowing hydrogen about 5,600 light-years away in Serpens, lit by the young cluster NGC 6611, whose most massive stars are only about 1.3 million years old. Their ultraviolet light is eroding the dark cloud they formed from, and has carved it into columns: the Pillars of Creation, which the Hubble telescope made famous in 1995, and the Spire. Stars are still forming inside them.
