A ring of glowing gas about 110 light-years across and about 4,700 light-years away in Monoceros, lit by the young cluster NGC 2244 in its hollow middle.

The Rosette Nebula is a ring of hydrogen about 110 light-years across, lit by the young cluster NGC 2244, whose hottest stars have blown a hollow in its middle. It’s about 4,700 light-years away. Its parts were found one at a time over the 19th century, and only Edward Barnard’s photographs of the early 1890s showed the whole ring.
