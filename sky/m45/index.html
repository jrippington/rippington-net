The Pleiades, the Seven Sisters, about 440 light-years away in Taurus: young stars about 120 million years old, drifting through a cloud of dust that reflects their blue light.

The Pleiades, M45, the Seven Sisters, are the nearest rich cluster of young stars, about 440 light-years away in Taurus and about 120 million years old. The blue nebula round them isn’t left over from their birth: the cluster is passing through a cloud of dust by chance, and the dust reflects the hot stars’ blue light in fine streaks that follow the Galaxy’s magnetic field. Their distance was argued over for years, until radio telescopes and Gaia settled it.
