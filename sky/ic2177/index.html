The Seagull Nebula, about 3,700 light-years away on the border of Monoceros and Canis Major: glowing gas like a gull in flight, its wings about 200 light-years long.

The Seagull Nebula, IC 2177, is a great sweep of glowing gas about 3,700 light-years away, on the border of Monoceros and Canis Major. Its head is a cloud lit by a young massive star, and its wings, about 200 light-years long, are gas at the edge of a cavity blown by earlier generations of stars, perhaps with supernovae among them, where new stars are still forming. Isaac Roberts photographed the head in 1898.
