The telescopes, Optica

iTelescope T9

A four-element refractor at Siding Spring

March 2022, 2 min read

On this page

  1. The telescope and the camera
  2. In numbers

T9 is a Tele Vue NP127fli, a 127 mm refractor made for imaging, with a large-format CCD camera, on a 10Micron mount at Siding Spring Observatory in Australia. Its frame is just over 3° square, about six full Moons across, wide enough for the larger nebulae and star clouds.

I started using iTelescope in 2022-2023, primarily to explore the southern skies while I was living in Central London. T9 is one of iTelescope’s telescopes at Siding Spring Observatory, in New South Wales, 31° south of the equator.

The telescope and the camera

The NP127fli is a Nagler-Petzval apochromat: four lenses, arranged so that the colours come to one focus and the field stays flat. iTelescope add Tele Vue’s Large Field Corrector behind it, to hold the stars sharp out to the corners of a big sensor, and they’ve replaced its focuser with a rigid adapter and a motorised FLI Atlas focuser, so nothing in the line from lens to camera can flex.

The camera is an FLI ProLine 16803, with the same 37 mm square KAF-16803 CCD as T8: 4096 pixels on a side, each 9 µm, covering 2.75 arcseconds at this focal length. The 10Micron mount tracks accurately enough that T9 runs without a guide camera.
