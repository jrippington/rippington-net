The shape of a fall

Near the Earth’s surface, ignoring air resistance and the slight weakening of gravity with height, an apple released from rest at height hhh above the floor is at height

y(t)=h−12gt2y(t) = h - \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2y(t)=h−21​gt2

a time ttt after it’s let go, where g≈9.8g \approx 9.8g≈9.8 metres per second per second. This is the parabola on the floor’s graph. Setting y=0y = 0y=0 gives the moment it reaches the floor:

t=2hg.t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}.t=g2h​​.

For Albert’s drop, from about 1.21.21.2 metres, that’s 2×1.2/9.8≈0.49\sqrt{2 \times 1.2 / 9.8} \approx 0.492×1.2/9.8​≈0.49 seconds, and the apple hits the floor at gt≈4.9g t \approx 4.9gt≈4.9 metres a second.

Seen from the falling probe, the same arithmetic runs the other way: the floor starts a distance hhh below the apple, and that distance shrinks as

h−12gt2,h - \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2,h−21​gt2,

the same expression, now describing the floor rising rather than the apple falling. Neither graph is wrong. Both are coordinate descriptions: descriptions in terms of particular rulers and clocks, the floor’s or the falling probe’s. The statement that doesn’t depend on any choice of rulers and clocks is the accelerometers’: the apple’s proper acceleration is zero, and the floor’s is ggg.

The metric

In empty space. A clock that moves a distance ddd while a time ttt passes on clocks standing still records only

τ=t2−d2c2\tau = \sqrt{t^2 - \frac{d^2}{c^2}}τ=t2−c2d2​​

of time. It is Pythagoras’s theorem with a minus sign where Pythagoras has a plus, and the minus sign is why a clock that travels records less. For the clock in the article, t=10t = 10t=10 seconds and d=6d = 6d=6 light-seconds, so d/c=6d/c = 6d/c=6 seconds and

τ=102−62=64=8 seconds.\tau = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = \sqrt{64} = 8 \text{ seconds}.τ=102−62​=64​=8 seconds.

For light, d=ctd = ctd=ct, and τ=0\tau = 0τ=0. Written for two nearby events, a small time dtdtdt and small distances dxdxdx, dydydy and dzdzdz apart, this is the metric of flat spacetime:

dτ2=dt2−dx2+dy2+dz2c2.d\tau^2 = dt^2 - \frac{dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2}{c^2}.dτ2=dt2−c2dx2+dy2+dz2​.

Near the Earth. Where gravity is weak, as it is round the Earth and the Sun, general relativity’s metric is, to a very good approximation,

dτ2=(1+2Φc2)dt2−(1−2Φc2)dℓ2c2,\begin{aligned} d\tau^2 = {} &\left(1 + \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\right) dt^2 \\ &- \left(1 - \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\right) \frac{d\ell^2}{c^2}, \end{aligned}dτ2=​(1+c22Φ​)dt2−(1−c22Φ​)c2dℓ2​,​

where dℓ2=dx2+dy2+dz2d\ell^2 = dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2dℓ2=dx2+dy2+dz2 is the square of the small distance between the two events, and Φ\PhiΦ is the gravitational potential of Time Runs Faster Upstairs, −GM/r-GM/r−GM/r at a distance rrr from the centre of a mass MMM. The first part says how fast clocks run at different places; the second, how gravity stretches space. For a clock standing still, the second part is zero, and

dτ=1+2Φc2  dt≈(1+Φc2)dt.d\tau = \sqrt{1 + \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}}\; dt \approx \left(1 + \frac{\Phi}{c^2}\right) dt.dτ=1+c22Φ​​dt≈(1+c2Φ​)dt.

Two clocks a height hhh apart near the ground differ in potential by ghghgh, so between the same two moments the higher one records about

1+ghc21 + \frac{gh}{c^2}1+c2gh​

times as much time as the lower one: 1+1.1×10−161 + 1.1 \times 10^{-16}1+1.1×10−16 for every metre.

Why the apple needs only the first part. For something moving at a speed vvv, the second part’s effect on its path is smaller than the first part’s by about v2/c2v^2/c^2v2/c2. For an apple falling at a few metres a second, that’s around 10−1610^{-16}10−16, so its path is set almost entirely by how fast clocks run at different heights, which is what the cone in the article was drawn to show. For light, v=cv = cv=c, and the two parts matter equally, which is why light passing the Sun is bent twice as much as the clock rates alone would bend it (the next journey).

The cone’s point. Each lap of the article’s cone is one tick of the clocks standing still at that height, so a lap at height hhh is shorter than the ground’s by the fraction gh/c2gh/c^2gh/c2. With the Earth’s real taper, the laps would shrink to nothing where gh/c2=1gh/c^2 = 1gh/c2=1:

h=c2g=(3.0×108)29.8≈9.2×1015 metres,\begin{aligned} h = \frac{c^2}{g} &= \frac{(3.0 \times 10^8)^2}{9.8} \\ &\approx 9.2 \times 10^{15} \text{ metres}, \end{aligned}h=gc2​​=9.8(3.0×108)2​≈9.2×1015 metres,​

about 0.97 light-years (a light-year is 9.46×10159.46 \times 10^{15}9.46×1015 metres). The Earth’s gravity weakens long before then; the point of the sum is how gentle the taper is.
