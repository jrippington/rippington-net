Why the distance shrinks

Here is a way to get the contraction from the light clock of the third journey, using a light clock laid lengthwise instead of upright.

Put a lamp and a detector at The Van’s back, and a mirror at its front, a proper distance L0L_0L0​ apart. A pulse of light goes from the back to the front and returns. Aboard The Van, the light goes L0L_0L0​ and comes back L0L_0L0​ at the speed of light ccc, so the round trip takes

τ=2L0c.\tau = \frac{2L_0}{c}.τ=c2L0​​.

Now look at the same round trip from a frame in which The Van moves forwards at speed vvv, and in which its length is LLL, whatever that turns out to be. On the way out, the front mirror is running away from the light, so the light closes on it at c−vc - vc−v and takes L/(c−v)L/(c - v)L/(c−v). On the way back, the back of The Van is coming to meet the light, which closes at c+vc + vc+v and takes L/(c+v)L/(c + v)L/(c+v). The round trip takes

t=Lc−v+Lc+v=2Lcc2−v2=2Lc⋅11−v2/c2.\begin{aligned} t &= \frac{L}{c - v} + \frac{L}{c + v} = \frac{2Lc}{c^2 - v^2} \\ &= \frac{2L}{c}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - v^2/c^2}. \end{aligned}t​=c−vL​+c+vL​=c2−v22Lc​=c2L​⋅1−v2/c21​.​

But the round trip begins and ends at the same clock, aboard The Van, so it is a single pair of events timed by a single clock, its proper time τ\tauτ, and time dilation says the other frame must measure it as γτ\gamma\tauγτ, where γ\gammaγ is the Lorentz factor:

t=γ 2L0c=2L0c⋅11−v2/c2.t = \gamma\,\frac{2L_0}{c} = \frac{2L_0}{c}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}.t=γc2L0​​=c2L0​​⋅1−v2/c2​1​.

Set the two expressions for ttt equal, and cancel the 2/c2/c2/c:

L1−v2/c2=L01−v2/c2,soL=L01−v2/c2=L0γ.\begin{gathered} \frac{L}{1 - v^2/c^2} = \frac{L_0}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}, \\ \text{so}\qquad L = L_0\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2} = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}. \end{gathered}1−v2/c2L​=1−v2/c2​L0​​,soL=L0​1−v2/c2​=γL0​​.​

At eight-tenths of the speed of light the square root is 0.6, and six metres becomes 3.6. The argument needs only algebra and the light clock’s result; the important detail is that the light starts and finishes at the same clock, so there is no argument about simultaneity in the timing.
