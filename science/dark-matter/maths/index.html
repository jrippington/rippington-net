Orbital speed and the mass inside

The pull a circle needs. Something going round a circle of radius rrr at speed vvv is turned aside continually, towards the centre, at

a=v2r.a = \frac{v^2}{r}.a=rv2​.

Twice as fast, and it needs four times the pull: twice as much speed to turn aside on every circle, in half the time. A circle twice as wide, at the same speed, needs half the pull.

Gravity supplies it. For a mass MMM inside the orbit (in the middle, or spread out in a ball: by Newton’s shell theorem the mass outside the orbit, if it’s spread evenly round it, pulls equally every way and cancels), gravity pulls with

a=GMr2,a = \frac{G M}{r^2},a=r2GM​,

a quarter as hard from twice as far. Setting the two equal gives the speed a circular orbit needs, and, turned round, the mass inside it:

v=GMr,M=v2rG.v = \sqrt{\frac{G M}{r}}, \qquad M = \frac{v^2 r}{G}.v=rGM​​,M=Gv2r​.

The planets. With the Sun’s mass in the middle, v∝r−1/2v \propto r^{-1/2}v∝r−1/2. From the Earth’s 29.78 km/s at 1 astronomical unit, Mercury at 0.387 should go at 29.78/0.387=47.929.78/\sqrt{0.387} = 47.929.78/0.387​=47.9 km/s (it goes at 47.4; its orbit is noticeably oval), Jupiter at 5.20 at 13.1 km/s, and Neptune at 30.07 at 5.43 km/s, as they do. Everything but the Sun adds up to about 0.13 per cent of its mass (Jupiter alone 0.095 per cent), so for the planets the Sun is all there is inside.

Neptune, too fast. At five times its speed, 27 km/s, Neptune would need 52=255^2 = 2552=25 times the pull, so 25 Suns’ worth of mass inside its orbit. With only one Sun, it’s well beyond the escape speed there, 2×5.43=7.7\sqrt{2} \times 5.43 = 7.72​×5.43=7.7 km/s, and leaves for good.

about 2×10272 \times 10^{27}2×1027 tonnes: two thousand trillion trillion.

A flat curve’s growing mass

Flat means growing. If the speed stays the same, vvv, at every distance, the mass inside each orbit is

M(r)=v2rG∝r:M(r) = \frac{v^2 r}{G} \propto r:M(r)=Gv2r​∝r:

twice as far, twice the mass. For a galaxy like Andromeda, at 230 km/s out to 100,000 light-years (r=30.7r = 30.7r=30.7 kpc),

M=2302×30.74.301×10−6≈3.8×1011 M⊙.M = \frac{230^2 \times 30.7}{4.301 \times 10^{-6}} \approx 3.8 \times 10^{11}\ M_\odot.M=4.301×10−62302×30.7​≈3.8×1011 M⊙​.

A ball of matter that gives a flat curve has a density falling as the square of the distance, ρ(r)=v2/4πGr2\rho(r) = v^2 / 4\pi G r^2ρ(r)=v2/4πGr2, so it goes on adding as much mass in each shell as in the last, out to wherever it ends.

The Milky Way. Dark matter in the Earth. Measured by the stars’ motions near the Sun, the dark matter here comes to about 0.3 to 0.5 GeV in each cubic centimetre, the mass of a third to a half of a proton. The Earth’s volume, 1.08×10211.08 \times 10^{21}1.08×1021 m³, holds about 0.6 to 1 kg of it at any moment, going through at a few hundred kilometres a second.

Weighing a cluster by its galaxies’ speeds

The balance of a swarm. A cluster that has settled down keeps a balance between the energy of its galaxies’ motion and the depth of its gravity (the virial theorem): 2K+U=02K + U = 02K+U=0. For a ball of mass MMM and radius RRR, evenly filled, U=−35GM2/RU = -\tfrac{3}{5} G M^2 / RU=−53​GM2/R, and if the galaxies move at ⟨v2⟩\langle v^2 \rangle⟨v2⟩ on average, K=12M⟨v2⟩K = \tfrac{1}{2} M \langle v^2 \rangleK=21​M⟨v2⟩, so

⟨v2⟩=35GMR,M=5σ2RG,\langle v^2 \rangle = \frac{3}{5} \frac{G M}{R}, \qquad M = \frac{5 \sigma^2 R}{G},⟨v2⟩=53​RGM​,M=G5σ2R​,

where σ\sigmaσ is the spread of speeds along the line of sight, the part the Doppler shift measures (⟨v2⟩=3σ2\langle v^2 \rangle = 3\sigma^2⟨v2⟩=3σ2).

Zwicky’s sum. In 1933 Zwicky took the Coma Cluster to be about 800 galaxies of 109 M⊙10^9\,M_\odot109M⊙​ each, in a ball about a million light-years across (306 kpc in radius, as he had it). Then

⟨v2⟩=35×4.301×10−6×8×1011306≈82 km/s,\sqrt{\langle v^2 \rangle} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{4.301 \times 10^{-6} \times 8 \times 10^{11}}{306}} \approx 82\ \text{km/s},⟨v2⟩​=53​×3064.301×10−6×8×1011​​≈82 km/s,

against the 1,000 km/s or so he measured. The pull needed grows as the square of the speed, so the cluster had to be about (1000/80)2≈160(1000/80)^2 \approx 160(1000/80)2≈160 times heavier than its light; with his own estimates, he wrote that it had to be at least 400 times denser.
