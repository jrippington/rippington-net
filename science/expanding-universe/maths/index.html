When Hubble’s law passes the speed of light

Hubble’s law is

v=H0D,v = H_0 D,v=H0​D,

where DDD is the galaxy’s distance, vvv is the rate at which that distance is growing, and H0H_0H0​, the Hubble constant, is today’s expansion rate, H0≈70H_0 \approx 70H0​≈70 kilometres a second per megaparsec. The recession rate reaches the speed of light, c≈299,792c \approx 299{,}792c≈299,792 kilometres a second, at

D=cH0≈299,792 km/s70 km/s per Mpc≈4,280 Mpc,D = \frac{c}{H_0} \approx \frac{299{,}792\ \text{km/s}}{70\ \text{km/s per Mpc}} \approx 4{,}280\ \text{Mpc},D=H0​c​≈70 km/s per Mpc299,792 km/s​≈4,280 Mpc,

and since a megaparsec is about 3.26 million light-years, that is about 14.0 billion light-years: the Hubble radius today. At other times the expansion rate is different, and is written HHH, the Hubble parameter; the Hubble radius at any time is c/Hc/Hc/H. (The time 1/H01/H_01/H0​, about 14 billion years, is close to the universe’s age of 13.8 billion years. That is partly a coincidence of the particular history our universe has had.)

Redshift and the universe’s scale

Write aaa for the scale factor, the universe’s scale: how big the distances between distant galaxies are, compared with the same distances today. Today a=1a = 1a=1; when aaa was 0.5, every such distance was half what it is now. And write zzz for the redshift: light that set out with a wavelength λthen\lambda_{\text{then}}λthen​ arrives with a wavelength λnow=(1+z) λthen\lambda_{\text{now}} = (1 + z)\,\lambda_{\text{then}}λnow​=(1+z)λthen​.

For a nearby galaxy, say 100 megaparsecs away, Hubble’s law gives 7,000 kilometres a second, and the redshift is close to v/cv/cv/c: about 0.023. That is the approximation Hubble used. For distant galaxies, the redshift is set by how much the universe grew during the light’s journey:

1+z=anowathen.1 + z = \frac{a_{\text{now}}}{a_{\text{then}}}.1+z=athen​anow​​.

A redshift of 1 means the universe has doubled in size since the light set out; a redshift of 3, that it has grown four times. Turning a redshift into a distance or a time needs a model of how aaa grew, which is what the article’s second and third figures compute. One more sign that a Doppler shift is the wrong idea: special relativity’s Doppler formula for light,

1+z=1+v/c1−v/c,1 + z = \sqrt{\frac{1 + v/c}{1 - v/c}},1+z=1−v/c1+v/c​​,

can never give a speed of light or more, whatever the redshift, while the measured universe is full of galaxies receding faster than that.

The horizons

The particle horizon is the same sum as the distance above, taken all the way back to the beginning, where the redshift has no limit. In the model (which, like the figures, leaves out radiation),
