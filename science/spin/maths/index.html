How the chance depends on the angle

As a wave. Light’s electric field, of size E0E_0E0​, shaking at an angle θ\thetaθ to a filter’s direction, can be split into a part along the filter, E0cos⁡θE_0\cos\thetaE0​cosθ, and a part across it, E0sin⁡θE_0\sin\thetaE0​sinθ. The filter passes the first and stops the second. A wave’s energy goes as the square of its size, so the share of the light’s energy that gets through is

II0=cos⁡2θ,\frac{I}{I_0} = \cos^2\theta,I0​I​=cos2θ,

Malus’s law (1809): all of it at θ=0\theta = 0θ=0, half at 45°45°45°, none at 90°90°90°. Light that is a jumble of every direction loses half at the first filter, since the average of cos⁡2θ\cos^2\thetacos2θ over all angles is 12\tfrac1221​.

As photons. A photon can’t be split, so the share becomes a chance: a photon polarised at θ\thetaθ to a filter gets through with probability cos⁡2θ\cos^2\thetacos2θ, and if it does, it comes out polarised along the filter. Over many photons the chances add up to Malus’s law.

Three filters. The first along 0°0°0°, the middle at θ\thetaθ, the last at 90°90°90°. Of the lamp’s photons,

12×cos⁡2θ×cos⁡2(90°−θ)=12cos⁡2θ sin⁡2θ=18sin⁡22θ\tfrac12 \times \cos^2\theta \times \cos^2(90° - \theta) = \tfrac12 \cos^2\theta\,\sin^2\theta = \tfrac18 \sin^2 2\theta21​×cos2θ×cos2(90°−θ)=21​cos2θsin2θ=81​sin22θ

reach the screen: at most 18\tfrac1881​, at θ=45°\theta = 45°θ=45°, a quarter of the 12\tfrac1221​ that got through the first. Without the middle filter, 12cos⁡290°=0\tfrac12 \cos^2 90° = 021​cos290°=0.

Why no sieve can do it. Suppose each photon carries a label λ\lambdaλ, anything at all, fixed at the lamp, and each filter passes it or not according to λ\lambdaλ alone, without changing it. Then the set of labels passing all three filters is contained in the set passing the first and the last, so

P(1, 2 and 3)≤P(1 and 3).P(\text{1, 2 and 3}) \le P(\text{1 and 3}).P(1, 2 and 3)≤P(1 and 3).

With the first and last crossed, the right-hand side is zero (nothing gets through two crossed filters), so the left-hand side would be zero too. It’s 18\tfrac1881​. So passing a filter must change something about the photon.

Many filters. Turn the polarisation through 90°90°90° with NNN filters, each 90°/N90°/N90°/N on from the last, and the share through all of them, of what passed the first, is (cos⁡290°N)N\left(\cos^2\frac{90°}{N}\right)^N(cos2N90°​)N: 14\tfrac1441​ for N=2N = 2N=2, 0.78 for N=10N = 10N=10, and closer and closer to 1 as NNN grows. Many small turns lose almost nothing.

An answer, asked again at an angle. In quantum mechanics, an electron found “up” along the vertical, asked again along a direction at an angle θ\thetaθ to it, is found along that direction with probability

P=cos⁡2θ2,P = \cos^2\frac{\theta}{2},P=cos22θ​,

and against it with probability sin⁡2(θ/2)\sin^2(\theta/2)sin2(θ/2). (Its state, up along the vertical, is cos⁡θ2 ∣↑θ⟩+sin⁡θ2 ∣↓θ⟩\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\,|{\uparrow_\theta}\rangle + \sin\frac{\theta}{2}\,|{\downarrow_\theta}\ranglecos2θ​∣↑θ​⟩+sin2θ​∣↓θ​⟩ in terms of up and down along the new direction, and the chances are the squares of the sizes of those arrows.) So: certain at 0°0°0°, half at 90°90°90°, never at 180°180°180°, which is the same question asked the other way round.

The half angle. For a photon, the chance is cos⁡2θ\cos^2\thetacos2θ: its opposite answer is a quarter turn away, and the even split at 45°45°45°. For spin it’s cos⁡2(θ/2)\cos^2(\theta/2)cos2(θ/2): the opposite answer is half a turn away, and the even split at 90°90°90°. Every angle in spin’s rules is half the angle in light’s, which is why the electron’s is called spin one half.

Three magnets. Up from the first; the middle at θ\thetaθ keeps a share cos⁡2(θ/2)\cos^2(\theta/2)cos2(θ/2) of them, along its own direction; the last finds those up with probability cos⁡2(θ/2)\cos^2(\theta/2)cos2(θ/2) and down with sin⁡2(θ/2)\sin^2(\theta/2)sin2(θ/2). At θ=90°\theta = 90°θ=90°, half are kept, and those come out half up, half down.
