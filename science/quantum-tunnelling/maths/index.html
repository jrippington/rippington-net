How the chance falls with thickness

A particle of mass mmm and energy EEE meets a wall of height VVV and thickness LLL. Its wave ψ(x)\psi(x)ψ(x) obeys Schrödinger’s equation,

−ℏ22m ψ′′(x)+V(x) ψ(x)=E ψ(x).-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\,\psi''(x) + V(x)\,\psi(x) = E\,\psi(x).−2mℏ2​ψ′′(x)+V(x)ψ(x)=Eψ(x).

Outside the wall, where V=0V = 0V=0, the solutions wave: ψ∝e±ikx\psi \propto e^{\pm ikx}ψ∝e±ikx with k=2mE/ℏk = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbark=2mE​/ℏ, the wave number of the fourth journey’s matter wave. Inside, where E<VE < VE<V, the equation reads ψ′′=κ2ψ\psi'' = \kappa^2 \psiψ′′=κ2ψ with

κ=2m(V−E)ℏ,\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(V - E)}}{\hbar},κ=ℏ2m(V−E)​​,

so the solutions don’t wave at all: they grow or fade, as e±κxe^{\pm \kappa x}e±κx.

Why it can’t stop dead. The equation says ψ′′\psi''ψ′′ is finite wherever VVV is, so ψ′\psi'ψ′ can have no jump and ψ\psiψ no corner, and certainly no step to zero. Put another way, the wave’s energy of motion is ℏ22m∫∣ψ′∣2 dx\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int |\psi'|^2\,dx2mℏ2​∫∣ψ′∣2dx, which a step in ψ\psiψ would make infinite. So at each face of the wall, ψ\psiψ and ψ′\psi'ψ′ must join smoothly onto the fading solution inside.

The exact answer. Matching ψ\psiψ and ψ′\psi'ψ′ at both faces, for a wave coming in from the left, gives the share that gets through:

T=[ 1+V2sinh⁡2(κL)4E (V−E) ]−1.T = \left[\,1 + \frac{V^2 \sinh^2(\kappa L)}{4E\,(V - E)}\,\right]^{-1}.T=[1+4E(V−E)V2sinh2(κL)​]−1.

(Above the top, E>VE > VE>V, the same working gives sin⁡\sinsin in place of sinh⁡\sinhsinh, with κ\kappaκ replaced by 2m(E−V)/ℏ\sqrt{2m(E - V)}/\hbar2m(E−V)​/ℏ: the share through is less than 1 unless LLL is a whole number of half wavelengths inside, which is why part of the wave is still turned back.)

Thick walls. Once κL\kappa LκL is more than about 1, sinh⁡κL≈12eκL\sinh \kappa L \approx \tfrac12 e^{\kappa L}sinhκL≈21​eκL, and

T≈16 EV(1−EV)e−2κL.T \approx 16\,\frac{E}{V}\left(1 - \frac{E}{V}\right) e^{-2\kappa L}.T≈16VE​(1−VE​)e−2κL.

The exponential is everything: each extra thickness δ\deltaδ multiplies TTT by the same factor e−2κδe^{-2\kappa\delta}e−2κδ, so doubling LLL squares the exponential.

Mass. At the same V−EV - EV−E, κ∝m\kappa \propto \sqrt{m}κ∝m​; a proton, 1,836 times as heavy as an electron, has κ\kappaκ larger by 1836=43\sqrt{1836} = 431836​=43.

Size and chance. The chance of finding the particle in a place goes as ∣ψ∣2|\psi|^2∣ψ∣2, so a wave on the far side half the height of the incoming one carries a quarter of the chance: TTT is the ratio of the squares.

Light across a gap

Light crossing from glass of refractive index nnn into air bends by Snell’s law, nsin⁡θ=sin⁡θairn \sin\theta = \sin\theta_\text{air}nsinθ=sinθair​. When nsin⁡θ>1n\sin\theta > 1nsinθ>1 there is no real θair\theta_\text{air}θair​: the light is totally reflected. For glass, n=1.5n = 1.5n=1.5, this happens beyond the critical angle

θc=arcsin⁡1n=41.8°.\theta_c = \arcsin\frac{1}{n} = 41.8°.θc​=arcsinn1​=41.8°.

The wave still reaches into the air. Along the surface it must match the light inside, which fixes the wave number along the surface at nsin⁡θ⋅2π/λn \sin\theta \cdot 2\pi/\lambdansinθ⋅2π/λ, more than the 2π/λ2\pi/\lambda2π/λ that light in air can have; so across the surface, into the air, it fades as e−z/δe^{-z/\delta}e−z/δ with

δ=λ2πn2sin⁡2θ−1,\delta = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\sqrt{n^2\sin^2\theta - 1}},δ=2πn2sin2θ−1​λ​,

exactly as the electron’s wave fades in its wall, with 2πn2sin⁡2θ−1/λ2\pi\sqrt{n^2\sin^2\theta - 1}/\lambda2πn2sin2θ−1​/λ playing the part of κ\kappaκ.

The glass of water. Looking down along the wall, the light in the water meets it at about 80°80°80° from the normal. The quantity nsin⁡θn\sin\thetansinθ along the surface is the same in every layer it crosses: 1.33×sin⁡80°=1.311.33 \times \sin 80° = 1.311.33×sin80°=1.31 in the water, so about 61°61°61° in the glass, beyond the critical angle into air. For green light, λ=550\lambda = 550λ=550 nm,

δ=550 nm2π1.312−1=550 nm2π×0.85≈100 nm,\delta = \frac{550\ \text{nm}}{2\pi\sqrt{1.31^2 - 1}} = \frac{550\ \text{nm}}{2\pi \times 0.85} \approx 100\ \text{nm},δ=2π1.312−1​550 nm​=2π×0.85550 nm​≈100 nm,

about a ten-thousandth of a millimetre, and the light’s intensity falls as e−2z/δe^{-2z/\delta}e−2z/δ. A fingerprint’s valleys stand off the glass by tens of micrometres, hundreds of times more, so they stay a mirror; where a ridge is in contact, or within a fraction of δ\deltaδ, the light gets out.

Radioactivity’s half-lives

Gamow’s picture: an alpha particle of energy QQQ inside a nucleus of radius RRR, and outside it the electric push of the rest of the nucleus, of charge ZdeZ_d eZd​e:

V(r)=2Zd×1.44 MeV fmr.V(r) = \frac{2 Z_d \times 1.44\ \text{MeV fm}}{r}.V(r)=r2Zd​×1.44 MeV fm​.

P=e−2G,G=1ℏ∫Rb2μ (V(r)−Q) dr,P = e^{-2G}, \qquad G = \frac{1}{\hbar}\int_R^b \sqrt{2\mu\,\big(V(r) - Q\big)}\,dr,P=e−2G,G=ℏ1​∫Rb​2μ(V(r)−Q)​dr,

G=2μQℏ b[arccos⁡x−x(1−x)],x=Rb,G = \frac{\sqrt{2\mu Q}}{\hbar}\, b\left[\arccos\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{x(1 - x)}\right], \qquad x = \frac{R}{b},G=ℏ2μQ​​b[arccosx​−x(1−x)​],x=bR​,

The Sun’s fusion

The hill. Two protons rrr apart have electric energy 1.44 MeV fm/r1.44\ \text{MeV fm}/r1.44 MeV fm/r: 720 keV at r0=2r_0 = 2r0​=2 fm, where the short-range pull takes over.
