Light’s speed from electricity and magnetism

Two electric charges, q1q_1q1​ and q2q_2q2​, a distance rrr apart, push or pull on each other with a force

F=14πε0 q1q2r2,F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2},F=4πε0​1​r2q1​q2​​,

where ε0≈8.854×10−12\varepsilon_0 \approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12}ε0​≈8.854×10−12 farads per metre is the electric constant: the first of the two numbers from the bench. Two long, straight, parallel wires carrying currents I1I_1I1​ and I2I_2I2​, a distance ddd apart, pull on each other (or push, if the currents run opposite ways) with a force, on each metre of wire, of

FL=μ02π I1I2d,\frac{F}{L} = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\,\frac{I_1 I_2}{d},LF​=2πμ0​​dI1​I2​​,

where μ0≈1.2566×10−6\mu_0 \approx 1.2566 \times 10^{-6}μ0​≈1.2566×10−6 newtons per square ampere is the magnetic constant: the second number.

Put their units together and μ0ε0\mu_0 \varepsilon_0μ0​ε0​ comes out in seconds squared per metre squared, so 1/μ0ε01/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}1/μ0​ε0​​ is a speed. Maxwell’s equations show why it matters. In empty space, with no charges or currents about, they combine into a wave equation for the electric field (and the same one for the magnetic field):

∂2E∂t2=1μ0ε0 ∂2E∂x2,\frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial t^2} = \frac{1}{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}\,\frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial x^2},∂t2∂2E​=μ0​ε0​1​∂x2∂2E​,

whose waves travel, without changing shape, at

c=1μ0ε0.c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}}.c=μ0​ε0​​1​.

With the numbers: μ0ε0=(1.25664×10−6)(8.85419×10−12)=1.11265×10−17\mu_0\varepsilon_0 = (1.25664 \times 10^{-6})(8.85419 \times 10^{-12}) = 1.11265 \times 10^{-17}μ0​ε0​=(1.25664×10−6)(8.85419×10−12)=1.11265×10−17 seconds squared per metre squared, whose square root is 3.33564×10−93.33564 \times 10^{-9}3.33564×10−9, so

c=13.33564×10−9=2.99792×108 metres a second.c = \frac{1}{3.33564 \times 10^{-9}} = 2.99792 \times 10^{8} \text{ metres a second}.c=3.33564×10−91​=2.99792×108 metres a second.

Weber and Kohlrausch, in 1856, measured an electric charge in two ways, by its electric force and by the magnetic force of the current when it flowed away, and found the ratio of the two to be 439,450 kilometres a second. In today’s units their ratio is 2 c\sqrt{2}\,c2​c, and 439,450/2=310,740439{,}450/\sqrt{2} = 310{,}740439,450/2​=310,740 kilometres a second: the number Maxwell compared with the speed of light.

The argument, in symbols. Suppose a laboratory moving steadily at velocity vvv measured the two constants and found different values, ε0′\varepsilon_0'ε0′​ and μ0′\mu_0'μ0′​, depending on vvv. Then c′=1/μ0′ε0′c' = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0'\varepsilon_0'}c′=1/μ0′​ε0′​​ would depend on vvv too, and measuring the constants on the bench would tell the laboratory its velocity: an experiment inside a steadily moving laboratory revealing its motion, which the principle of relativity forbids. So ε0′=ε0\varepsilon_0' = \varepsilon_0ε0′​=ε0​, μ0′=μ0\mu_0' = \mu_0μ0′​=μ0​, and c′=cc' = cc′=c, in every inertial frame. The only escape is a frame in which the constants take their “true” values and others in which they don’t: the ether’s, which experiment has never found.

(Since 2019, the SI units fix ccc exactly, and μ0\mu_0μ0​ and ε0\varepsilon_0ε0​ are measured, with μ0ε0c2=1\mu_0\varepsilon_0 c^2 = 1μ0​ε0​c2=1 by definition. The physics is the same: the speed of electromagnetic waves and the strength of electric and magnetic forces are tied together by Maxwell’s equations.)

Michelson and Morley’s race

A swimmer who swims at speed sss through the water, in a river flowing at vvv, swims a distance LLL downstream and back in

t∥=Ls+v+Ls−v=2Lss2−v2.t_\parallel = \frac{L}{s + v} + \frac{L}{s - v} = \frac{2Ls}{s^2 - v^2}.t∥​=s+vL​+s−vL​=s2−v22Ls​.

To swim straight across, the swimmer must aim upstream, so that the current’s push is cancelled, leaving a speed across of s2−v2\sqrt{s^2 - v^2}s2−v2​, and the trip across and back takes

t⊥=2Ls2−v2.t_\perp = \frac{2L}{\sqrt{s^2 - v^2}}.t⊥​=s2−v2​2L​.

For the swimmers in the article, L=100L = 100L=100 metres, s=2s = 2s=2 and v=1v = 1v=1 metres a second: t∥=400/3≈133t_\parallel = 400/3 \approx 133t∥​=400/3≈133 seconds and t⊥=200/3≈115t_\perp = 200/\sqrt{3} \approx 115t⊥​=200/3​≈115 seconds.

For light, s=cs = cs=c and vvv is the Earth’s speed through the ether. With β=v/c\beta = v/cβ=v/c, which is small (about 10−410^{-4}10−4 for the Earth’s speed round the Sun),

t∥≈2Lc(1+β2),t⊥≈2Lc(1+12β2),t_\parallel \approx \frac{2L}{c}\left(1 + \beta^2\right), \qquad t_\perp \approx \frac{2L}{c}\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta^2\right),t∥​≈c2L​(1+β2),t⊥​≈c2L​(1+21​β2),

so the beam along the wind is late by Δt≈(L/c) β2\Delta t \approx (L/c)\,\beta^2Δt≈(L/c)β2. Turning the apparatus through a right angle swaps the arms’ roles, which changes the difference by 2Δt2\Delta t2Δt. Counted in waves of light of wavelength λ\lambdaλ (in time, λ/c\lambda/cλ/c each), the stripes should shift by

n=2Δtλ/c=2Lβ2λ.n = \frac{2\Delta t}{\lambda / c} = \frac{2L\beta^2}{\lambda}.n=λ/c2Δt​=λ2Lβ2​.

With Michelson and Morley’s 11 metres, β=10−4\beta = 10^{-4}β=10−4 and yellow light, λ≈5.5×10−7\lambda \approx 5.5 \times 10^{-7}λ≈5.5×10−7 metres: n≈2×11×10−8/(5.5×10−7)=0.4n \approx 2 \times 11 \times 10^{-8} / (5.5 \times 10^{-7}) = 0.4n≈2×11×10−8/(5.5×10−7)=0.4 of a stripe. They saw no more than a twentieth of that.

The Doppler effect in numbers

Sound. A source sends a crest every TTT seconds (its frequency is f=1/Tf = 1/Tf=1/T) and moves at speed uuu through still air, in which sound goes at sss (343 metres a second). Ahead of it, each crest starts uTuTuT closer to the one before, so the crests are (s−u)T(s - u)T(s−u)T apart and arrive every (s−u)T/s(s - u)T/s(s−u)T/s seconds. A still listener ahead hears

fahead=f1−u/s,fbehind=f1+u/s.f_{\text{ahead}} = \frac{f}{1 - u/s}, \qquad f_{\text{behind}} = \frac{f}{1 + u/s}.fahead​=1−u/sf​,fbehind​=1+u/sf​.

For an ambulance at 30 metres a second, u/s=0.0875u/s = 0.0875u/s=0.0875: ahead, f/0.9125=1.096ff/0.9125 = 1.096ff/0.9125=1.096f, nearly 10 per cent higher; behind, f/1.0875=0.920ff/1.0875 = 0.920ff/1.0875=0.920f, about 8 per cent lower. The pitch falls by a factor of 1.096/0.920=1.1921.096/0.920 = 1.1921.096/0.920=1.192 as it passes, which is 12log⁡21.192≈312\log_2 1.192 \approx 312log2​1.192≈3 semitones.

If instead the source is still and the listener moves towards it at uuu, the crests are sTsTsT apart, but the listener meets them at s+us + us+u:

flistener moves=f(1+us).f_{\text{listener moves}} = f\left(1 + \frac{u}{s}\right).flistener moves​=f(1+su​).

At half the speed of sound, a moving source is heard at 2f2f2f (an octave up) but a moving listener hears only 1.5f1.5f1.5f: who moves through the air matters.

Light. Relative to the probe, a lamp approaching at v=βcv = \beta cv=βc sends each flash from where it is, so the crowding is the same as for sound, a factor of 1/(1−β)1/(1 - \beta)1/(1−β). Adding velocities without breaking light

Galileo’s rule for adding velocities along a line is the one everyone uses without thinking: if something moves at speed uuu relative to The Van, and The Van moves at speed vvv relative to the probe, then relative to the probe it moves at a speed www:

w=u+v.w = u + v.w=u+v.

Einstein’s rule, which comes from the slowed clocks, shortened rulers and shifted moments of the next few journeys, is

w=u+v1+uv/c2.w = \frac{u + v}{1 + uv/c^2}.w=1+uv/c2u+v​.

At everyday speeds, the bottom of that fraction is so close to 1 that the two rules agree. For the ball on the train, u=10u = 10u=10 and v=30v = 30v=30 metres a second, and

uvc2=300(3×108)2≈3×10−15,\frac{uv}{c^2} = \frac{300}{(3 \times 10^8)^2} \approx 3 \times 10^{-15},c2uv​=(3×108)2300​≈3×10−15,

a difference of about three parts in a thousand million million: far too small for anyone on the train, or beside it, to notice.

At high speeds the rules part company. Two speeds of half the speed of light don’t make the speed of light:

w=0.5c+0.5c1+0.25=0.8c.w = \frac{0.5c + 0.5c}{1 + 0.25} = 0.8c.w=1+0.250.5c+0.5c​=0.8c.

And for light from The Van’s headlights, u=cu = cu=c, with The Van at any speed vvv at all:

w=c+v1+v/c=c+v(c+v)/c=c.w = \frac{c + v}{1 + v/c} = \frac{c + v}{(c + v)/c} = c.w=1+v/cc+v​=(c+v)/cc+v​=c.

However fast the lamp, Einstein’s rule gives c. For now, take the rule on trust: it is the arithmetic of a world in which everyone measures the same speed for light.
