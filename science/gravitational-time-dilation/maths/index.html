Nanoseconds on a mountain

The Van gives us the size of the effect with nothing more than the rules for speed.

A flash takes a time h/ch/ch/c to climb a height hhh. While it climbs, The Van, accelerating at ggg, gains speed:

Δv=g×hc.\Delta v = g \times \frac{h}{c}.Δv=g×ch​.

For speeds small compared with light, the Doppler effect changes a frequency by the fraction Δv/c\Delta v / cΔv/c. So the flashes arrive stretched by the fraction

Δff≈Δvc=ghc2.\frac{\Delta f}{f} \approx \frac{\Delta v}{c} = \frac{gh}{c^2}.fΔf​≈cΔv​=c2gh​.

By the equivalence principle, the same fraction applies between two clocks a height hhh apart near the Earth. It’s an approximation, good for heights small enough that gravity is about the same at the top as at the bottom.

Notice that the units cancel: metres per second squared, times metres, divided by metres squared per second squared, leaves a pure number, a fraction, as a rate difference should be.

For h=2000h = 2000h=2000 metres and g≈9.8g \approx 9.8g≈9.8 metres per second per second:

Δff≈9.8×2000(3.0×108)2≈2.2×10−13.\frac{\Delta f}{f} \approx \frac{9.8 \times 2000}{(3.0 \times 10^8)^2} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-13}.fΔf​≈(3.0×108)29.8×2000​≈2.2×10−13.

Over one day, the higher clock gains that fraction of 86,400 seconds:

2.2×10−13×86,400 s≈1.9×10−8 s,2.2 \times 10^{-13} \times 86{,}400\ \text{s} \approx 1.9 \times 10^{-8}\ \text{s},2.2×10−13×86,400 s≈1.9×10−8 s,

about 19 nanoseconds. Over a year, about 7 microseconds. That’s an illustration of the right size for a couple of kilometres of height, not a measured value for the café.

  • For one metre, gh/c2=9.8/(9.0×1016)≈1.1×10−16gh/c^2 = 9.8 / (9.0 \times 10^{16}) \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-16}gh/c2=9.8/(9.0×1016)≈1.1×10−16: about one part in ten million billion. For The Van’s six metres, 6.5×10−166.5 \times 10^{-16}6.5×10−16.
  • A million flashes, a second apart, sent up two kilometres: 106 s×2.2×10−13≈2.2×10−710^6\ \text{s} \times 2.2 \times 10^{-13} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-7}106 s×2.2×10−13≈2.2×10−7 seconds, about a fifth of a microsecond more by the top clock’s count.
  • A good quartz watch, wrong by about half a second a day, drifts by the fraction 0.5/86,400≈5.8×10−60.5 / 86{,}400 \approx 5.8 \times 10^{-6}0.5/86,400≈5.8×10−6: about 25 million times the mountain’s 2.2×10−132.2 \times 10^{-13}2.2×10−13.
  • Eighty years is about 2.5×1092.5 \times 10^92.5×109 seconds. Gravitational potential

The gravitational potential Φ\PhiΦ is the energy per kilogram that it takes to lift something from one place to another, counted from some chosen level. Near the ground, lifting a kilogram by a height hhh takes g×hg \times hg×h joules, so between two heights

ΔΦ=gh,\Delta \Phi = gh,ΔΦ=gh,

and the clocks’ rates differ by the fraction

Δff≈ΔΦc2,\frac{\Delta f}{f} \approx \frac{\Delta \Phi}{c^2},fΔf​≈c2ΔΦ​,

which near the ground is the gh/c2gh/c^2gh/c2 of the mountain. With c2≈9.0×1016c^2 \approx 9.0 \times 10^{16}c2≈9.0×1016 square metres per second squared (about 90 million billion), each metre adds about 1.1×10−161.1 \times 10^{-16}1.1×10−16.

Further out, gravity weakens. Its strength a distance rrr from the centre of the Earth is GM/r2GM/r^2GM/r2, where GM≈3.986×1014GM \approx 3.986 \times 10^{14}GM≈3.986×1014 cubic metres per second squared for the Earth, and the potential is

Φ(r)=−GMr,\Phi(r) = -\frac{GM}{r},Φ(r)=−rGM​,

lowest deep in the well and rising to zero infinitely far away. Close to the surface, for a height hhh small compared with the Earth’s radius RRR, the difference is Φ(R+h)−Φ(R)≈GMh/R2=gh\Phi(R + h) - \Phi(R) \approx GMh/R^2 = ghΦ(R+h)−Φ(R)≈GMh/R2=gh: the rule for mountains, where every metre counts the same. Further up, each metre counts for less. A GPS satellite, about 26,560 kilometres from the centre of the Earth, feels gravity weaker than ours by the factor (6,371/26,560)2≈1/17(6{,}371 / 26{,}560)^2 \approx 1/17(6,371/26,560)2≈1/17.

Out of the well altogether, at r→∞r \to \inftyr→∞, a clock runs faster than one on the ground by the fraction

GMRc2=3.986×10146.371×106×8.988×1016≈7.0×10−10,\begin{aligned} \frac{GM}{R c^2} &= \frac{3.986 \times 10^{14}}{6.371 \times 10^{6} \times 8.988 \times 10^{16}} \\ &\approx 7.0 \times 10^{-10}, \end{aligned}Rc2GM​​=6.371×106×8.988×10163.986×1014​≈7.0×10−10,​

seven parts in ten billion.

The satellites’ clocks

A GPS satellite’s clock gains on a clock on the ground because it is higher up, and loses because it is moving.

Height. Between the ground, at R=6,371R = 6{,}371R=6,371 kilometres from the centre of the Earth, and the orbit, at r=26,560r = 26{,}560r=26,560 kilometres, the difference in potential, divided by c2c^2c2, is

GMc2(1R−1r)≈6.96×10−10−1.67×10−10=5.3×10−10.\begin{aligned} &\frac{GM}{c^2}\left(\frac{1}{R} - \frac{1}{r}\right) \\ &\approx 6.96 \times 10^{-10} - 1.67 \times 10^{-10} \\ &= 5.3 \times 10^{-10}. \end{aligned}​c2GM​(R1​−r1​)≈6.96×10−10−1.67×10−10=5.3×10−10.​

(Allowing for the Earth’s shape and spin, as the GPS engineers do, gives 5.30×10−105.30 \times 10^{-10}5.30×10−10.) Over a day, 5.30×10−10×86,400≈45.85.30 \times 10^{-10} \times 86{,}400 \approx 45.85.30×10−10×86,400≈45.8 microseconds gained.

Speed. In a circular orbit the speed is v=GM/r≈3,874v = \sqrt{GM/r} \approx 3{,}874v=GM/r​≈3,874 metres a second, about 13,950 kilometres an hour. At speeds much less than light’s, a moving clock runs slow by the fraction v2/2c2v^2 / 2c^2v2/2c2, from the Lorentz factor of A Clock Made of Light:

v22c2=3,87422×8.988×1016≈0.835×10−10,\begin{aligned} \frac{v^2}{2c^2} &= \frac{3{,}874^2}{2 \times 8.988 \times 10^{16}} \\ &\approx 0.835 \times 10^{-10}, \end{aligned}2c2v2​​=2×8.988×10163,8742​≈0.835×10−10,​

or about 7.2 microseconds lost in a day.

Together, the satellite’s clock runs fast by

5.30×10−10−0.835×10−10≈4.465×10−10,\begin{aligned} &5.30 \times 10^{-10} - 0.835 \times 10^{-10} \\ &\approx 4.465 \times 10^{-10}, \end{aligned}​5.30×10−10−0.835×10−10≈4.465×10−10,​

about 38.6 microseconds a day, in which a radio signal travels 38.6×10−6×3.0×108≈11.638.6 \times 10^{-6} \times 3.0 \times 10^{8} \approx 11.638.6×10−6×3.0×108≈11.6 kilometres. It is the 446.5 parts in a million million that general relativity predicted for NTS-2. To cancel it, the clock is set before launch to run slow by the same fraction:

10.23 MHz×(1−4.465×10−10)=10.22999999543 MHz.\begin{aligned} &10.23\ \text{MHz} \times \left(1 - 4.465 \times 10^{-10}\right) \\ &= 10.22999999543\ \text{MHz}. \end{aligned}​10.23 MHz×(1−4.465×10−10)=10.22999999543 MHz.​
