The real thing. The balance isn’t kept, for two reasons Jim Peebles worked out in 1968. An electron caught straight into hydrogen’s lowest level gives out a photon big enough to free another, so that capture achieves nothing; and an electron caught into the second level can only get down by giving out Lyman-alpha light, which the next atom along absorbs at once. Most atoms actually form by a slow way round: an electron in the second level’s spherical state drops to the lowest by giving out two photons at once, which happens about 8.2 times a second. Solving Peebles’s equation (with the recombination rate of Pequignot, Petitjean and Boisson, 1991, and the correction of Seager, Sasselov and Scott’s RECFAST, 1999; helium left out), as the article’s figure does, gives half the electrons free at about 3,470 K (z≈1270z \approx 1270z≈1270, at about 285,000 years), and about one in seven, xe=0.145x_e = 0.145xe​=0.145, at 3,000 K (z=1100z = 1100z=1100). The light’s last bounces come thickest at z≈1080z \approx 1080z≈1080, when the universe was about 377,000 years old. (Planck’s fuller calculation, with helium, gives z∗=1090z_* = 1090z∗​=1090.)

Why it fails. In a universe that doesn’t change, the glow’s temperature is the same at every time. In an expanding one, T=T0(1+z)T = T_0(1+z)T=T0​(1+z). The recipe

The critical amount. In general relativity, a universe with as much matter and energy as

ρc=3H028πG=8.5×10−27 kg/m3,\rho_c = \frac{3H_0^2}{8\pi G} = 8.5 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg/m}^3,ρc​=8πG3H02​​=8.5×10−27 kg/m3,

about five hydrogen atoms’ worth in each cubic metre, is flat. More curves it like a ball; less, like a saddle. So flat means Ωtotal=1\Omega_\text{total} = 1Ωtotal​=1.

What it’s made of. The ripples’ pattern measures the ordinary matter (from how the peaks alternate in height, the matter’s weight deepening the fog’s squeezes) and the dark matter (from how the pattern’s overall shape and the third peak need extra matter that doesn’t feel the light). Planck’s 2018 results:

Ωb=0.049,Ωc=0.266,ΩΛ=1−Ωm−Ωr=0.685,\Omega_b = 0.049, \qquad \Omega_c = 0.266, \qquad \Omega_\Lambda = 1 - \Omega_m - \Omega_r = 0.685,Ωb​=0.049,Ωc​=0.266,ΩΛ​=1−Ωm​−Ωr​=0.685,

with radiation Ωr=9×10−5\Omega_r = 9 \times 10^{-5}Ωr​=9×10−5 today. The usual round figures, about 5, 27 and 68 per cent, are Planck’s first results of 2013 (4.9, 26.8 and 68.3). Dark matter is 0.266/0.049≈5.40.266/0.049 \approx 5.40.266/0.049≈5.4 times the ordinary matter.
