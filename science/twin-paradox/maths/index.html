Proper time along each path

For a clock moving at a steady speed vvv relative to an inertial frame, for a time Δt\Delta tΔt of that frame, the proper time it measures is

Δτ=Δt 1−v2c2=Δtγ.\Delta\tau = \Delta t\,\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} = \frac{\Delta t}{\gamma}.Δτ=Δt1−c2v2​​=γΔt​.

At v=0.8cv = 0.8cv=0.8c the square root is 1−0.64=0.36=0.6\sqrt{1 - 0.64} = \sqrt{0.36} = 0.61−0.64​=0.36​=0.6, so the Lorentz factor is γ=1/0.6=5/3\gamma = 1/0.6 = 5/3γ=1/0.6=5/3. Each five-year leg in the home station’s frame is three years aboard. Two legs: six years, against ten at home, with the turn treated as instantaneous.

The same thing written as spacetime geometry, with c=1c = 1c=1 (years and light-years), is

Δτ2=Δt2−Δx2,\Delta\tau^2 = \Delta t^2 - \Delta x^2,Δτ2=Δt2−Δx2,

and for each leg, Δt=5\Delta t = 5Δt=5 and Δx=4\Delta x = 4Δx=4, so Δτ2=25−16=9\Delta\tau^2 = 25 - 16 = 9Δτ2=25−16=9, and Δτ=3\Delta\tau = 3Δτ=3. The minus sign is the reason the straight path is the longest: any Δx\Delta xΔx makes Δτ\Delta\tauΔτ smaller than Δt\Delta tΔt.

Rounding the turn

The figure’s rounded turn is a steady push as The Van’s crew feel it: a constant proper acceleration aaa, the acceleration The Van’s accelerometer reads. In years and light-years, with c=1c = 1c=1, one g is a=1.03a = 1.03a=1.03 light-years per year per year.

Under a steady proper acceleration, the easiest thing to follow is the rapidity ϕ\phiϕ, defined by v=ctanh⁡ϕv = c\tanh\phiv=ctanhϕ, because it grows in step with the traveller’s own clock: dϕ/dτ=a/cd\phi/d\tau = a/cdϕ/dτ=a/c. In the home station’s frame, γ=cosh⁡ϕ\gamma = \cosh\phiγ=coshϕ and γv=csinh⁡ϕ\gamma v = c\sinh\phiγv=csinhϕ, so the traveller’s time, home’s time and the distance covered change by

Δτ=ca Δϕ,Δt=ca Δ(sinh⁡ϕ),Δx=c2a Δ(cosh⁡ϕ).\Delta\tau = \frac{c}{a}\,\Delta\phi, \qquad \Delta t = \frac{c}{a}\,\Delta(\sinh\phi), \qquad \Delta x = \frac{c^2}{a}\,\Delta(\cosh\phi).Δτ=ac​Δϕ,Δt=ac​Δ(sinhϕ),Δx=ac2​Δ(coshϕ).

Home’s time, assigned and seen

The leap at the turn comes from the relativity of simultaneity. A frame moving at vvv gives events at home, a distance Δx=4\Delta x = 4Δx=4 light-years behind it, times that differ from the home station’s own reckoning by vΔx/c2=0.8×4=3.2v\Delta x/c^2 = 0.8 \times 4 = 3.2vΔx/c2=0.8×4=3.2 years. On the way out, The Van’s “now” at home is 3.2 years behind the home station’s “now” at the turn (5−3.2=1.85 - 3.2 = 1.85−3.2=1.8 years); on the way back, moving the other way, it’s 3.2 years ahead (5+3.2=8.25 + 3.2 = 8.25+3.2=8.2). The leap is 6.4 years.

On each coasting leg, The Van’s frame has home’s clock running slow by γ\gammaγ, so in The Van’s three years it assigns home 3/γ=3×0.6=1.83/\gamma = 3 \times 0.6 = 1.83/γ=3×0.6=1.8 years: from 0 to 1.8 on the way out, and from 8.2 to 10 on the way back. By The Van’s bookkeeping, 1.8+6.4+1.8=101.8 + 6.4 + 1.8 = 101.8+6.4+1.8=10.

And the light arriving: the Doppler factor for light, received frequency over sent frequency, is

1−v/c1+v/c=0.21.8=13\sqrt{\frac{1 - v/c}{1 + v/c}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.2}{1.8}} = \frac{1}{3}1+v/c1−v/c​​=1.80.2​​=31​

going away, and its reciprocal, 3, coming back. Three years out at a third of the rate shows one year of home; three years back at three times the rate shows nine.
