How many atoms are in a grain of sugar

Take a grain of granulated sugar as a cube half a millimetre on each side. Its volume is

V=(0.5×10−3 m)3=1.25×10−10 m3.V = (0.5 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m})^3 = 1.25 \times 10^{-10}\ \text{m}^3.V=(0.5×10−3 m)3=1.25×10−10 m3.

Sugar (sucrose) has a density of about 1,590 kilograms per cubic metre, so the grain weighs

m=1590×1.25×10−10≈2.0×10−7 kg,m = 1590 \times 1.25 \times 10^{-10} \approx 2.0 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{kg},m=1590×1.25×10−10≈2.0×10−7 kg,

about a fifth of a milligram. A molecule of sucrose, C12H22O11\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11}C12​H22​O11​, weighs 342.3 atomic mass units, so a mole of it (Avogadro’s number of molecules, 6.022×10236.022 \times 10^{23}6.022×1023) weighs 342.3 grams. The grain holds

Nmolecules=0.20×10−3 g342.3 g/mol×6.022×1023≈3.5×1017N_\text{molecules} = \frac{0.20 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{g}}{342.3\ \text{g/mol}} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} \approx 3.5 \times 10^{17}Nmolecules​=342.3 g/mol0.20×10−3 g​×6.022×1023≈3.5×1017

molecules, and at 45 atoms each,

Natoms≈1.6×1019.N_\text{atoms} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{19}.Natoms​≈1.6×1019.

Counted at one a second, that’s 1.6×10191.6 \times 10^{19}1.6×1019 seconds, or about 5×10115 \times 10^{11}5×1011 years: about 36 times the age of the universe, 13.8 billion years.

Why only a tiny centre can turn a particle round

Two charges q1q_1q1​ and q2q_2q2​ a distance rrr apart push or pull on each other with a force (Coulomb’s law)

F=k q1q2r2,F = \frac{k\, q_1 q_2}{r^2},F=r2kq1​q2​​,

where k=1/(4πε0)≈8.99×109k = 1/(4\pi\varepsilon_0) \approx 8.99 \times 10^9k=1/(4πε0​)≈8.99×109 in SI units. For particle physics it’s handier to write ke2=1.44k e^2 = 1.44ke2=1.44 MeV femtometres, where eee is the charge of a proton and a femtometre is 10−1510^{-15}10−15 metres.

Head on. An alpha particle has charge 2e2e2e; a gold nucleus has 79e79e79e. Fired straight at the nucleus with kinetic energy EEE, the alpha particle slows as it climbs the electric “hill”, and stops where its kinetic energy has all become electric potential energy:

E=k (2e)(79e)d⇒d=2×79×1.44 MeV fmE.E = \frac{k\,(2e)(79e)}{d} \quad\Rightarrow\quad d = \frac{2 \times 79 \times 1.44\ \text{MeV fm}}{E}.E=dk(2e)(79e)​⇒d=E2×79×1.44 MeV fm​.

For alpha particles of 5.5 MeV, about the energy of those from radium, d≈41d \approx 41d≈41 fm. That’s how close it gets before being turned back.

Off centre. Passing at a distance bbb from the nucleus’s line (its impact parameter), the particle follows a hyperbola and is turned through an angle θ\thetaθ, where

tan⁡θ2=d2b.\tan\frac{\theta}{2} = \frac{d}{2b}.tan2θ​=2bd​.

Head on (b=0b = 0b=0), θ=180°\theta = 180°θ=180°: straight back. To be turned through more than a right angle, it must pass within b=d/2≈21b = d/2 \approx 21b=d/2≈21 fm of a nucleus.

How often. A foil of thickness ttt with nnn nuclei per cubic metre presents, to the beam, a target of area πb2\pi b^2πb2 round each nucleus. The share of particles passing within b=d/2b = d/2b=d/2 of one is

P>90°≈n t π(d2)2.P_{>90°} \approx n\, t\, \pi \left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^2.P>90°​≈ntπ(2d​)2.

For gold, n=5.9×1028n = 5.9 \times 10^{28}n=5.9×1028 per cubic metre; for a foil 0.4 micrometres thick,

P>90°≈5.9×1028×4×10−7×π×(21×10−15)2≈3×10−5,P_{>90°} \approx 5.9 \times 10^{28} \times 4 \times 10^{-7} \times \pi \times (21 \times 10^{-15})^2 \approx 3 \times 10^{-5},P>90°​≈5.9×1028×4×10−7×π×(21×10−15)2≈3×10−5,

about one in thirty thousand. Rutherford’s paper of 1911 gives about one in twenty thousand for the gold foils of Geiger and Marsden’s experiments. The rarity measures how small the target is.

The pudding. Spread the same charge ZeZeZe evenly through a ball of radius RRR, the size of the atom. Inside a uniform ball of charge, the electric push at distance rrr from the centre grows only in proportion to rrr, and its component across a straight path at distance bbb from the centre is the same all along the path. An alpha particle crossing the ball at speed vvv, along a chord of length 2R2−b22\sqrt{R^2 - b^2}2R2−b2​, gets a sideways kick of

Δp=k (2e)(Ze) bR3×2R2−b2v,\Delta p = \frac{k\,(2e)(Ze)\, b}{R^3} \times \frac{2\sqrt{R^2 - b^2}}{v},Δp=R3k(2e)(Ze)b​×v2R2−b2​​,

and, since its momentum is p=mvp = mvp=mv and its energy E=12mv2E = \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2E=21​mv2, it is turned through

θ≈Δpp=dR⋅bR1−b2R2.\theta \approx \frac{\Delta p}{p} = \frac{d}{R} \cdot \frac{b}{R} \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{R^2}}.θ≈pΔp​=Rd​⋅Rb​1−R2b2​​.

That is largest for a pass at b=R/2b = R/\sqrt{2}b=R/2​, where θ=d/2R\theta = d/2Rθ=d/2R. A gold atom is about R=1.44×10−10R = 1.44 \times 10^{-10}R=1.44×10−10 m in radius, so

θmax=41×10−152×1.44×10−10≈1.4×10−4 radians≈0.008°.\theta_\text{max} = \frac{41 \times 10^{-15}}{2 \times 1.44 \times 10^{-10}} \approx 1.4 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{radians} \approx 0.008°.θmax​=2×1.44×10−1041×10−15​≈1.4×10−4 radians≈0.008°.

Many nudges. Gold leaf 0.4 micrometres thick is about 1,400 atoms thick. Random kicks add like a random walk: the typical total grows only as the square root of their number, so 1400≈37\sqrt{1400} \approx 371400​≈37 kicks’ worth, a fraction of a degree. (The electrons add a little more, for a total of about a degree.) The chance of random kicks adding to 90°90°90° behaves like e−(90°/1°)2/2e^{-(90°/1°)^2/2}e−(90°/1°)2/2, which is so small that no number of alpha particles in the history of the universe would show it once.

How small the nucleus is

Rutherford’s bound. An alpha particle sent straight back has come within d≈41d \approx 41d≈41 fm of the centre, and the push on it obeyed Coulomb’s law all the way in. So the nucleus’s charge must be packed inside a radius smaller than that, about 4×10−144 \times 10^{-14}4×10−14 metres, while the atom is about 10−1010^{-10}10−10 metres across: thousands of times smaller, at least.

Today’s measurements. The proton, hydrogen’s nucleus, has a radius of about 0.84 fm. A hydrogen atom’s size is set by the Bohr radius, a0=5.29×10−11a_0 = 5.29 \times 10^{-11}a0​=5.29×10−11 m. The ratio is

a0rp=5.29×10−118.4×10−16≈63,000.\frac{a_0}{r_p} = \frac{5.29 \times 10^{-11}}{8.4 \times 10^{-16}} \approx 63{,}000.rp​a0​​=8.4×10−165.29×10−11​≈63,000.

Scale the proton up to a grain of sugar half a millimetre across, and the atom becomes 0.5 mm×63,000≈310.5\ \text{mm} \times 63{,}000 \approx 310.5 mm×63,000≈31 metres across: its edge about 16 metres from the grain.

The mass. A proton weighs 1,836 times as much as an electron, so the nucleus carries

18361837≈99.95 per cent\frac{1836}{1837} \approx 99.95\ \text{per cent}18371836​≈99.95 per cent

of a hydrogen atom’s mass.

How long a planet-style atom lasts

The orbit. An electron (charge −e-e−e, mass mmm) going round a proton in a circle of radius rrr needs the electric pull to supply the force for circular motion:

ke2r2=mv2r⇒v2=ke2mr.\frac{k e^2}{r^2} = \frac{m v^2}{r} \quad\Rightarrow\quad v^2 = \frac{k e^2}{m r}.r2ke2​=rmv2​⇒v2=mrke2​.

It goes round f=v/2πrf = v / 2\pi rf=v/2πr times a second,

f=12πke2mr3,f = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k e^2}{m r^3}},f=2π1​mr3ke2​​,

which at the Bohr radius is about 6.6×10156.6 \times 10^{15}6.6×1015 times a second. Its total energy, motion plus electric, is

U=−ke22r:U = -\frac{k e^2}{2r}:U=−2rke2​:

the lower the orbit, the more negative, so losing energy means sinking.

about sixteen trillionths of a second. Light travels cT≈4.7cT \approx 4.7cT≈4.7 mm in that time: a grain of sugar half a millimetre across about ten times.

The whistle. The light comes out at the orbit’s frequency. At the start that’s 6.6×10156.6 \times 10^{15}6.6×1015 Hz, a wavelength of c/f≈46c/f \approx 46c/f≈46 nanometres, in the far ultraviolet. Since f∝r−3/2f \propto r^{-3/2}f∝r−3/2, the frequency climbs as the orbit shrinks: at a tenth of the starting radius the light has a wavelength of about 1.4 nanometres, in X-rays.
