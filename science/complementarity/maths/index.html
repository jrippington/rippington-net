Why a record stops the arrows adding

No record. With nothing keeping a record, the electron’s amplitude to arrive at xxx is the sum of the amplitudes of its two ways, ψ(x)=ψA(x)+ψB(x)\psi(x) = \psi_A(x) + \psi_B(x)ψ(x)=ψA​(x)+ψB​(x), and the chance (as the last journey’s maths page showed) is

P(x)=∣ψA(x)∣2+∣ψB(x)∣2+2 Re[ψA∗(x) ψB(x)],P(x) = |\psi_A(x)|^2 + |\psi_B(x)|^2 + 2\,\text{Re}\big[\psi_A^*(x)\,\psi_B(x)\big],P(x)=∣ψA​(x)∣2+∣ψB​(x)∣2+2Re[ψA∗​(x)ψB​(x)],

the last term making the stripes.

A record. Now let the probe, or anything else, end up in a state that depends on the way: ∣a⟩|a\rangle∣a⟩ if the electron went through A, ∣b⟩|b\rangle∣b⟩ if through B, each normalised, ⟨a∣a⟩=⟨b∣b⟩=1\langle a|a\rangle = \langle b|b\rangle = 1⟨a∣a⟩=⟨b∣b⟩=1. The electron and the record together are in the state

∣Ψ⟩=ψA(x) ∣a⟩+ψB(x) ∣b⟩.|\Psi\rangle = \psi_A(x)\,|a\rangle + \psi_B(x)\,|b\rangle.∣Ψ⟩=ψA​(x)∣a⟩+ψB​(x)∣b⟩.

The chance of finding the electron at xxx, whatever the record says, is the sum over every possible reading of the record, which comes to

P(x)=∣ψA(x)∣2+∣ψB(x)∣2+2 Re[ψA∗(x) ψB(x) ⟨a∣b⟩].P(x) = |\psi_A(x)|^2 + |\psi_B(x)|^2 + 2\,\text{Re}\big[\psi_A^*(x)\,\psi_B(x)\,\langle a|b\rangle\big].P(x)=∣ψA​(x)∣2+∣ψB​(x)∣2+2Re[ψA∗​(x)ψB​(x)⟨a∣b⟩].

The interference term is multiplied by the overlap of the two records, ⟨a∣b⟩\langle a|b\rangle⟨a∣b⟩. If the records are perfectly different, ⟨a∣b⟩=0\langle a|b\rangle = 0⟨a∣b⟩=0, the two endings are as different as two places on the screen, and the term vanishes: the chances add, P=∣ψA∣2+∣ψB∣2P = |\psi_A|^2 + |\psi_B|^2P=∣ψA​∣2+∣ψB​∣2, and there are no stripes. If the records are identical, ∣a⟩=∣b⟩|a\rangle = |b\rangle∣a⟩=∣b⟩, nothing tells the ways apart, and the stripes are whole. In between, the stripes are weakened by the factor ∣⟨a∣b⟩∣|\langle a|b\rangle|∣⟨a∣b⟩∣.

Nothing here depends on anyone reading the record: the sum over readings has already been done. What matters is only whether the records differ.

Dimmer light. If a fraction fff of the electrons scatter a photon (and are recorded perfectly) and the rest scatter none,

P(x)=f (∣ψA∣2+∣ψB∣2)+(1−f) ∣ψA+ψB∣2,P(x) = f\,\big(|\psi_A|^2 + |\psi_B|^2\big) + (1 - f)\,|\psi_A + \psi_B|^2,P(x)=f(∣ψA​∣2+∣ψB​∣2)+(1−f)∣ψA​+ψB​∣2,

a smooth patch from the seen electrons and full stripes from the unseen ones, laid on top of each other. Sorted by the probe’s log, the two separate exactly, as Cal found.

Kicks. A random kick would shift each electron’s stripes by a random amount, and enough randomness would smear them out. Dürr, Nonn and Rempe’s path marker, a pulse of microwaves (about 3 GHz) flipping the atom’s inner state, carries a momentum hf/chf/chf/c far too small to shift the stripes appreciably; yet the stripes vanished, as the overlap ⟨a∣b⟩=0\langle a|b\rangle = 0⟨a∣b⟩=0 of the two inner states says they should.

Distinguishability and the stripes’ strength

For two ways of equal strength, the stripes’ strength (their visibility, the swing from bright to dark as a fraction of the average) is the size of the records’ overlap:

V=∣⟨a∣b⟩∣.V = |\langle a|b\rangle|.V=∣⟨a∣b⟩∣.

How well the record can tell. The best any reading of the record can do, guessing A or B, is to be right with probability (Helstrom’s bound)

Pright=12(1+1−∣⟨a∣b⟩∣2).P_\text{right} = \tfrac{1}{2}\Big(1 + \sqrt{1 - |\langle a|b\rangle|^2}\Big).Pright​=21​(1+1−∣⟨a∣b⟩∣2​).

Write the distinguishability D=2Pright−1D = 2P_\text{right} - 1D=2Pright​−1, which runs from 0 (a coin toss) to 1 (always right). Then D=1−∣⟨a∣b⟩∣2D = \sqrt{1 - |\langle a|b\rangle|^2}D=1−∣⟨a∣b⟩∣2​, and

D2+V2=1.D^2 + V^2 = 1.D2+V2=1.

When the probe’s records are partly noisy as well, the equality becomes an inequality, D2+V2≤1D^2 + V^2 \leq 1D2+V2≤1: the duality relation, from work by Wootters and Zurek (1979), Greenberger and Yasin (1988) and Englert (1996). A probe right nine times in ten has D=0.8D = 0.8D=0.8, so V≤0.6V \leq 0.6V≤0.6: stripes at 60 per cent at best.
