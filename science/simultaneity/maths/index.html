How the present tilts

Two inertial frames moving at speed vvv relative to each other, along the line joining the events, relate the times of events by the Lorentz transformation, named, like the Lorentz factor of the third journey, after Hendrik Lorentz:

t′=γ(t−v xc2).t' = \gamma\left(t - \frac{v\,x}{c^2}\right).t′=γ(t−c2vx​).

Here ttt and xxx are an event’s time and position in one frame, t′t't′ its time in the other, and γ\gammaγ is the Lorentz factor. Suppose two events are simultaneous in the first frame, so the time between them is Δt=0\Delta t = 0Δt=0, but they are a distance Δx\Delta xΔx apart. Subtract the transformed times of the two events:

Δt′=−γ v Δxc2.\Delta t' = -\gamma\,\frac{v\,\Delta x}{c^2}.Δt′=−γc2vΔx​.

The time between them in the second frame isn’t zero, unless v=0v = 0v=0 or Δx=0\Delta x = 0Δx=0. That is the relativity of simultaneity in one line. The minus sign says which comes first: in a frame moving towards the event that is further along (larger xxx), that event comes first.

At The Station, Δx\Delta xΔx is the length of the girder, which light crosses in 40 microseconds, so Δx/c\Delta x/cΔx/c is 40 microseconds; v/c=0.6v/c = 0.6v/c=0.6; and γ=1.25\gamma = 1.25γ=1.25. So

Δt′=−1.25×0.6×40 μs=−30 μs.\Delta t' = -1.25 \times 0.6 \times 40\ \mu\text{s} = -30\ \mu\text{s}.Δt′=−1.25×0.6×40 μs=−30 μs.

B flashed 30 microseconds before A, in The Van’s frame: 15 before noon and 15 after, as Albert’s clocks said.

The same transformation explains the warning about the drawings. For any two events, take the time between them as the distance light covers in it, c Δtc\,\Delta tcΔt, square it, and subtract the square of the distance between them. (This is why spacetime diagrams, the article’s among them, often label the time axis ctctct: the time multiplied by the speed of light, which turns time into the distance light covers in it.) Every inertial frame gets the same answer:

(c Δt′)2−Δx′2=(c Δt)2−Δx2.(c\,\Delta t')^2 - \Delta x'^2 = (c\,\Delta t)^2 - \Delta x^2.(cΔt′)2−Δx′2=(cΔt)2−Δx2.

For the two flashes, The Station’s frame gives 0−1220 - 12^20−122, and The Van’s gives 92−1529^2 - 15^292−152 (thirty microseconds of light is 9 kilometres, and the flashes were seven and a half kilometres either side of John): minus 144 square kilometres, both times. On a map, Pythagoras adds the squares; here they subtract. That is why a tick of 20 microseconds on The Van’s tilted time axis lies further out on the page than the same tick on The Station’s upright one. The second figure draws faint curves, called hyperbolae, through every event 20 or 40 microseconds before or after the moment John passed the middle, as any frame’s own clock would measure it, and through the matching distances to either side: however the axes tilt, their ticks land on the same curves.

Andromeda’s four days

The same formula shows why nobody notices the relativity of simultaneity in everyday life, and why it matters across a galaxy. At everyday speeds γ\gammaγ is 1 to many decimal places, so two events that are simultaneous in one frame are, in a frame moving at vvv, a time

Δt′≈vc×Δxc\Delta t' \approx \frac{v}{c} \times \frac{\Delta x}{c}Δt′≈cv​×cΔx​

apart: the speed as a fraction of light’s, multiplied by the time light takes to cross the distance between them.

Across the café terrace. Two clocks about 20 metres apart, and someone passing at the speed of a racing car, 100 metres a second: v/c≈3.3×10−7v/c \approx 3.3 \times 10^{-7}v/c≈3.3×10−7, and light crosses 20 metres in about 6.7×10−86.7 \times 10^{-8}6.7×10−8 seconds, so their “now”s differ by about 2×10−142 \times 10^{-14}2×10−14 seconds, twenty millionths of a billionth of a second.

Across to Andromeda. John strolls at 5 kilometres an hour, 1.39 metres a second, so v/c≈4.6×10−9v/c \approx 4.6 \times 10^{-9}v/c≈4.6×10−9. Light takes about two and a half million years to cross to Andromeda. So the events at Andromeda that John’s frame calls “now” are later than the ones Cal’s frame calls “now” by 4.6×10−9×2.5×1064.6 \times 10^{-9} \times 2.5 \times 10^{6}4.6×10−9×2.5×106 years, which is about 0.0116 years, or 4.2 days.
